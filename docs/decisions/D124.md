# D124 · A page tells what its kind is for, and each claim of it comes with its laws

Domain. Language.
Decision. A page says two things, each once. Its prose tells what the
name is for and how it relates to the rest, with its links; its claims
tell how it behaves, each a line of prose ending in a colon followed by
the laws that prove it:

      A key orders by what it answers for each element:
      ~([{:age 30} {:age 20}] | sort ~(/age) | eq [{:age 20} {:age 30}])

A caption is the last sentence of the prose before a law when it ends
in a colon, its lines joined as one, so a claim may take several lines;
it holds for every law below it until a blank line or another caption. A law with no caption stays a law.
`runLaws` answers `{:law :says :actual :ok :error}` per law, `:says`
the caption it proves or null, so a failure reads as the claim that
broke. A behaviour the prose tells and a claim proves is told by the
claim alone.
