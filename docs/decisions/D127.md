# D127 · A refusal says the form a reader meant

Domain. Language.
Decision. A refusal that meets a form a reader writes by habit names the
form that is right. A combinator written after a pipe, `a | * b` or
`a | !| b`, is refused by the parser with the sentence that a combinator
stands in the place of `|`, `a * b`, where it listed the tokens the
parser could take. A tag name whose path names no verb, `::any/links`,
is refused with the addresses where a verb of its last name lives,
`::doc/links`, among its `:addresses`. And the page of verbs says that a
kind is declared by the step `::T body`, the tag, a space and the body,
while `::T{…}` written close is a value under the tag and declares
nothing, which a reader cannot tell apart by the result alone. `*`
keeps one spelling: no verb `each` beside it, since `::each` is the
kind of the step `*` writes and a verb of that name would give one name
two kinds again [D115].
Source. «а если  для | * - сделать | each ... плохая идея?»
(maintainer, 2026-09-28 19:57, session d427ce7e); «ок, правильное
обучающее сообщение ошибки тоже хороших сам по себе сигнал... и остальне
делай» (20:01), after runs R6 to R8 of the benchmark each met `| *`, R8
a guessed address with no hint and R7 and R8 a kind written close to its
body. The sentences and the reading of the space are the model's, the
same day.
Set aside. A verb `each` beside `*`, a second spelling of one fact under
the name of a step. A refusal of `::T{…}` under a tag no declaration
binds, since a tag goes over any payload without one.
