# D133 · `coalesce` takes a fallback value, the subject first

Domain. Language.
Decision. An alternative of `coalesce` is a quote or a value of any
kind, `* :alternatives #[::quote ::any]`. A quote runs against the
subject, as before; a value answers itself. A call whose alternatives
are all values puts the subject first, so `at :timeout | coalesce 30`
reads "the timeout, else 30" and `[1] | coalesce []` keeps `[1]`. A call
with a quote among its alternatives leaves the subject out, and
`{:nickname null :name "alice"} | coalesce ~(/nickname) ~(/name)` reads
as it did.
Source. «т.е. если можно не тянуть исключения в стартовое описание и не
воевать с прайором - а просто дешево полечить в самом языке - то так и
надо делать» (maintainer, 2026-10-02 23:10, session 87118b06). Clean
readers wrote `x | coalesce d`, the `x ?? d` of other languages,
more often than any other refused form. Sonnet wrote it in three of the
four tasks it missed blind on a fresh set, and nobody had shown it the
form.
Set aside. A value alternative that answers itself with the subject
left out, under which `[1] | coalesce []` answers `[]` without a
refusal. The subject as the first candidate of every call, which turns
`{:a 1} | coalesce ~(/b) ~(/c)` into the map itself. A second verb for
the fallback, which readers would not reach for.
Replaces in part [D77], whose alternatives were code alone.
