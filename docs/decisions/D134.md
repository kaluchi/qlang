# D134 · A projection written before a word names the verb it meant

Domain. Language.
Decision. A step that is a projection, followed by whitespace and a word
that is not a combinator, is refused by the parser with the verb the
reader meant: `filter ~(/ne "")` answers "`/ne` reads the key ne; a verb
is called by its name alone, `ne …`". A projection as a modifier,
`mul /qty /price`, and a projection before a combinator read as they
did.
Source. «если видишь что проблему с непониманием языка новенькими можно
решить добавлением операнда или переименованием его - то так и делай ..»
(maintainer, 2026-10-02 23:08, session 87118b06). Haiku, working a fresh
set of tasks with the command line, wrote `/ne ""`, `/startsWith "^"`
and `/contains "=="`, the method call of other languages, in four of the
seven tasks it missed. The parser named the quote after the word and
listed the tokens it expected, and the reader never found its way back
to the verb.
Set aside. Reading `/ne ""` as the call `ne ""`, a second spelling of
one call that the reader would carry into a modifier, where `/ne` is a
key.
