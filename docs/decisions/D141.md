# D141 · `trim`, and the first page says why a step sees one value

Domain. Language.
Decision. `trim`, a verb of `::string`, answers the text without the
whitespace at its two ends. The first page states the concept the
modifiers follow, where it listed none: a step sees one value, the one
the step before handed it, and everything inside the step, its modifiers
among them, is computed from that value, so what an earlier step saw is
gone unless a name keeps it. The behaviour of one operand under that
concept is told on the operand's own pages: the page of
`::AtKeyNotKeywordOrStringError` says that `at /email` hands `at` the
value of the field as its key, where `at :email` names the key.
Source. «трим добавь  ...at тоже можно доработать ошибку, что б яснее
была.. и может как-то первичное описание доработать чуть.. как проекция
работает или как шаги считаются..» (maintainer, 2026-10-03 17:43, session
87118b06); «прям расписывать на стартовом экране как ведут себя операнды
- это грязь.. там должна быть видна концепция, почему так вычисляется ..
почему такое поведение» (17:47). On a fresh set of tasks Haiku, writing
blind, wrote `at /key` in five of the eleven tasks it missed and read a
modifier against a subject an earlier step had changed in four, and
wrote `trim`, which the language lacked.
Set aside. The behaviour of `at` on the first page, an operand's detail
on the screen of concepts; a refusal of `at /key`, whose key read from
the record is a form the language keeps.
