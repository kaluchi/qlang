# D146 · What the first page and a refused call tell a newcomer

Domain. Language.
Decision. The first page states the reading of a missing part as the
language makes it: a part read strictly, `/k` or `/0`, fails where it is
missing, and one read softly, `at`, `first`, `last`, `min` or `max`,
answers null; it lists `payload` as the verb that opens a value under a
tag, apart from the verbs of every value. A call that reaches the
contract of its name, the kind of its subject serving no verb of it,
carries `:actualType`, the kind, beside `:addresses`, and its page says
so from the reader's side: the kind of the subject has no verb of the
name, and the addresses name the kinds whose verbs answer it.
Source. «да, хорошие замечания 1 и 2 и 3» (maintainer, 2026-10-03 20:38,
session 87118b06), on a clean Sonnet's reading of the core after it
solved a fresh set with the command line. It found `payload` among the
verbs of every value, where `5 | payload` is refused; `no default for a
missing part` on the first page, where `[] | first` answers null; and
`1 | count` answered with a page about a verb without a body, where the
reader needs to hear that a number has no `count`.
Set aside. A refusal of its own per kind and verb, `CountSubjectNot…`,
which the contract already answers by its addresses; and soft verbs
made strict, which would break the readings `at` and `first` exist for.
