# D152 · One tree of names: a name is declared by a keyword, and a tag is its address

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The maintainer, the tree of names, a declaration by its
keyword and a declared name a step can call; the reading of the probes,
what a step can call, the place of a label and of a name under a noun,
and the wording of the refusals are the model's.
Decision. The names of the language form one tree, and every node of it
is a binding: a name, its page and its value. What the node is, its
value says: a page makes a label, a quote or a host's primitive under a
label is a word, and any other value is data. A declaration names its
node by a keyword alone, `:number |~~ … ~~|`; a tag name in the place
of a declaration's name is refused, so `::Box []` declares nothing. The
keyword names what a step can call: a bare name, a letter or `_` and what
follows, or names joined by `/`, so `:"first name" 5` and `:"1" 5` are
refused by the parser at the keyword, which names nothing a step can
call, and `:"foo" 5` declares `foo`, the quotes around a bare name
changing nothing. The word of a literal, `true`, `false` or `null`,
declares no value or word alone, since a step reads it as the literal,
`:true 5` refused; it names a label, which its tag reaches, `::null`, and
the last name under a noun, which its address calls, `number/null`. A
tag name is the address of a node, and `manifest` answers the nodes
under a noun, its words, its refusals and its pages. A tag no
declaration names lays itself over a payload as it is, with no page and
no check.
