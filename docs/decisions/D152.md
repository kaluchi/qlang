# D152 · One tree of names: a name is declared by a keyword, and a tag is its address

Domain. Language.
Decision. The names of the language form one tree, and every node of it
is a binding: a name, its page and its value. What the node is, its
value says: a page makes a label, a quote or a host's primitive under a
label is a word, and any other value is data. A declaration names its
node by a keyword alone, `:number |~~ … ~~|`; a tag name in the place
of a declaration's name is refused, so `::Box []` declares nothing. A
tag name is the address of a node, and `manifest` answers the nodes
under a noun, its words, its refusals and its pages. A tag no
declaration names lays itself over a payload as it is, with no page and
no check.

Set aside. A name declared by its tag beside the names declared by a
keyword, two forms of one binding whose key the scope spells `::Box`.
A module that declares its own noun under a role, `:noun`, an exception
to the rule of addresses that only the core's two collisions asked for.
A refusal addressed from the root of its provider, which keeps the
nodes of a module out of the tree its noun heads and makes `manifest`
answer the exports of a module apart from the tree of names. A short
form of a provider's tag found by its uniqueness, whose meaning a
module loaded later changes. A keyword with a path as the name of a
module, a second name of what its tag already addresses.
