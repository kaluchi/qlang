# D168 · `::spec` and `::verb` leave the language, a page is prose about its name, and what lies under an address is its definition, read and assembled on demand

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The maintainer.
Decision. The tags `::spec` and `::verb` leave the language whole: a
kind is a label, a name declared by its page, and a reader's verb is a
name under the label of its subject, `:number/inc ~(add 1)`, as the
catalog's are [D164]. No node is an exception to the rule of labels: a
page declared under `::any` is a label like any other, and the contract
of a word, a verb without a body there, leaves with `::verb`.
A page, the doc in the slot of a declaration, is prose about the name
it is declared with, the links and the snippets it names among its
prose; the laws a page carried become definitions of their own under
the address they concern, as `construct` is one. Every definition is a
name, its page and its value, and the value is simple, neither a
filler like `::spec~()` nor a head; the definitions under a label's
address are the whole description of what stands under it. An
invariant has a name, as a refusal has one beside the place it guards,
and a value that breaks the invariant is refused under that name: the
invariant and its refusal are one definition.
A description is assembled on demand: the definitions under an address
are scanned, selected by what their values are and recomposed into the
answer a question needs, in one round trip, and everything written
under an address is read at once as the source written there, never a
table of contents that leaves the reader to read on, nor a bare
literal, nor a set of narrow reads each a step of its own.
Documentation is read in two ways, independent tasks one of which may
rely on what the other provides: the legend of a value, its print
annotated, and the peek into a label a reader met as a tag in a printed
value, which holds without a value to annotate, `::vec` among them.
A name is the strongest signal a reader gets, so every name says what
it holds: `isA` is named again, `is`.
