# D174 · A law is a definition under the name it concerns, named a law by every word that reads it, `laws` and `runLaws`

Decision. A law is a definition under the address whose behaviour it
proves: its name says the claim, its page is the sentence of the claim,
and its value is `::law~(…)`, a query that answers true,
`:vec/take/takesNothingByANegativeCount |~~ A negative count takes
nothing. ~~| ::law~([1 2 3] | take -1 | eq [])`. It stands under the
address beside its parts, its answer and its refusals, the kind of each
value saying what each is [D169]. Every word that reads a law names it
a law, and none reads it under a second name: `laws` answers the laws
under the name a value reads, as `doc` reads it, each by its page and
its query, in the order of the declarations, so a reader meets the
claims and their code in one answer; `runLaws` runs the laws of a name,
each result naming the law by its address and its claim by its page.
`manifest` lists the anatomy of a name, its parts, its answer, its
refusals and the names under it, and leaves its laws to `laws`,
`::vec/take | manifest | has ::vec/take/takesNothingByANegativeCount`
answering `false`. A page holds the prose about its name [D168], and a
sentence that states the rule a law is an instance of stays prose in
it, the chapters of `::qlang` among them.
