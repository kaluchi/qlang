# D179 · A definition introduces its name as a story of statements, and the names it introduces are the words its prose uses

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The maintainer.
Decision. A reader falls into a definition through the editor or
`source` with questions: what the name takes, what it does with a value
of another kind, with several values or with none, what it answers. The
definition answers them where the reader lands, a story of statements
that introduces the name: what it is, what it is for, what it works
with, what it answers and how it behaves. The definitions under a word
stand in the order of that story: the word and its page; its answer,
under the key `answer`; its parts; its refusals, each a page that says
when and how to repair it; its laws, one for each question the page
settles, the false and the refused among them. A name the story brings
in as a key is a word its prose uses, so a page says what a word
answers where the key is `answer`. The catalog is re-sorted and its
texts written again to that form, `eq` the first of them; a summary
assembled from the catalog as it stands, a synopsis, a usage or a
tutorial over it, is no substitute for that work.

Set aside. A synopsis, a usage or a tutorial assembled over a catalog
in disorder, which has to search what no order holds. Names of answers
chosen word by word, `sum`, `taken`, `same`, `greater`, which the eye
learns anew under every word.
Requirements. The cases that name D179, as each definition is written
to the form.
