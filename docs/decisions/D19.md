# D19 · A doc is the markdown of the language, the vector of the prose and the quotes the parser reads in it under `::doc`, and answers its text and its parts by verbs of its own

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The maintainer; the parser's reading of the segments and a
doc built back by `tag` are the model's.
Decision. A doc is the markdown of the language, prose that holds
quotes of qlang: the vector of its segments, prose strings and quotes,
under `::doc`, written `|~~ … ~~|`. The parser reads the segments where
it reads the literal, so no second reading of a doc's text is left to
the runtime: a `~(…)` whose text reads as code is a quote, under the
tags written before it where it has them, `::link~(…)` [D108]; a span
between backquotes is prose whole; and the rest is prose, a tag written
in it among it, `|~~ a `~(add 1)` b ~~| | segments | count` answering
`1`. What a query asks of a doc is asked of its noun: `content` answers
its text, each quote written where it stands, and `segments` the vector
of its parts, `|~~ a ~(add 1) b ~~| | segments` answering
`[" a " ~(add 1) " b "]`; further verbs of a doc join them in its source
when a task asks for them. `tag ::doc` builds a doc from a vector of
strings and quotes and refuses any other element,
`[" a " 1] | tag ::doc` answering `::doc/ElementNotSegmentError`, so a
doc comes apart into its segments and back by `tag`, as a quote does
[D42]. The shape under the tag is the doc's own: its verbs are what a
doc answers, and the verbs of vectors that reach it through its payload
[D34] are no part of that. `join` is the vector's verb alone, so
`42 | join ","` is the vector's refusal [D34].
