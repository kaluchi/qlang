# D217 · Within a budget a part gives way only where its marker prints shorter

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The model, at the maintainer's word to decide on the way.
Decision. `elide` gives way, among the parts beneath the top, only a
part whose marker of it whole prints shorter than it: `[]`, `{:n 1}`
and a short string stay as they are, so a budget never lengthens a
print, and a value whose parts all print no longer than their markers
is answered as it is, over the budget. The largest such part gives way
first: a string or a map to its marker, a vector to its first half and
a marker of the rest, halving on to its marker whole at the most, which
prints shorter than it. An alert's excerpt and a budget the command
line asks for follow the same rule, as a subject a failure passed gives
way only to a shorter marker [D184].
Source. Testing [D214], the command line under `--budget=200` printed a
failure in 431 characters where its alert printed whole took 317:
`:nearest []` given way to `::elision{:size 0 :read ~(!| /nearest)}`
and the subject `{:n 1}` to a marker five times its length. The laws of
`elide` showed the mechanism on strings shorter than their markers, so
the defect was the rule they held. «сразу решай все по пути»
(maintainer, 2026-10-11 20:17, session 22b934f8).
Set aside. Each step of the halving allowed only where it prints
shorter, which stops a vector at once, since a marker among its elements
spreads the vector over lines, and gives forty numbers way whole where
five of them and a marker fit; a floor of size below which nothing gives
way, a number with no reason.
Replaces in part [D109], under which the largest part gave way whatever
its marker printed.
Requirements. `:any/elide/keepsAPartShorterThanItsMarker`, `[[] {:n 1}
"short"] | elide 3` answering itself; the laws of a string giving way on
a string longer than its marker; the unit test that a failure within a
budget prints no longer than its alert whole.
