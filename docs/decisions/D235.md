# D235 · An example holds code under a bare name, and declares a word only where it shows a word

Domain. Catalog.
Decided by. The maintainer.
Decision. An example of a page, a law of the catalog and the showcase of
the language server hold code under a bare name and run it by `apply`,
or hand it bare to a word that takes code, as [D44] has it:
`:double ~(mul 2) | 5 | apply double` answers `10`,
`:double ~(mul 2) | [1 2 3] | map double` answers `[2 4 6]`, and the
name alone reads its quote, `:double ~(mul 2) | 5 | double` answering
`~(mul 2)`. A law that needs a name to hold its claims holds them under
a value of the reader, `:k 5 | :k/answersFive |~~ … ~~| ::law~(k | eq 5)`.
An example declares a word under a label only where it shows a word: a
call by its name on the subject, its parts and their invariants, `~name`
in the place of a quote, the short form of a fold, a recursion by name,
a value meeting a word, a noun of the root extended. The word then goes
under the label of the values it serves, a word that doubles a number
under `::number` or under a label of the reader's own, and `::any` keeps
the words that mean the same for every value.
