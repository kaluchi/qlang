# D251 · A quote is data, a recipe reads what its call hands it, and a function is built from the noun `::fn` by commands

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The maintainer: a quote pure data, with no scope of its own
and no verb, a verb belonging to its subject and a quote taking part in
its recipe, which a host's primitive may be instead; a value a word's
recipe needs taken as a part or written in it; a function built by
commands from the noun `::fn`, `takes`, `returns`, `throws` and its
recipe last by `impl`, a word written so with no group around its chain;
every name with its doc and its value, an input's kind among what
`takes` takes; and `fn.qlang` writing its own words as their literal.
The model's: the scope a recipe and the code it runs read, the literal
`::fn[…]`, `apply` handing the objects after a function as its
arguments, `fill` and `params`, an input's place as its value, the
answer as a name of the signature, a body begun on a later line read as
a sentence, and a doc as the object of a command.
Decision. A quote is data: its steps and nothing else, so two quotes
`eq` holds of answer alike wherever they run, and
`:f (:k 10 | :g ~(mul k) | g) | :k 2 | :h ~(mul k) | [(f | eq h) (3 | apply f) (3 | apply h)]`
answers `[true 6 6]`. A word is a node under the label of its subject,
and its recipe, a quote or a host's primitive, runs in the scope its
call opens: its parts and `args`, the names its steps declare, and the
tree, the words and the nodes of the root and those the query declared
with a path or as a label. No value a sentence names reaches a recipe,
where the word was declared or where it is called, so
`:n 3 | :number/times ~(mul n) | 5 | times` is refused, and a value a
recipe needs is a part, with a default where a call may leave it out,
or is written in it. A quote written in the step that runs it runs in
the scope of that step: `[1 2] | :k 10 | map ~(mul k)` answers
`[10 20]`, and the step `map ~(add by)` of a recipe reads the part
`by`. Any other code a verb runs, a quote held in a name or a part or
read from a page, runs in the tree of names of the query, the scope of
the reader's step its call began from, and no name a recipe writes, a
part, `args`, a name its steps declare, reaches it [D200]: a quote
handed to a word written in the language reads the names of its
author, though a part of the word bear one of them, and
`:f (:k 10 | ~(mul k)) | 3 | apply f` is refused, no step of its query
having named `k`.

A function is a value of the kind `::fn`, built from the noun by
commands, its own words taking the tag as their subject [D88]:
`takes :by |~~ How much. ~~| /0 ::number` adds an input, its name, its
page, its place among the arguments of a call, `/0` or `~(query /0 1)`,
as a part has it, and its kind where one is given;
`returns |~~ The subject and by added. ~~| ::number` its answer, with
its page and its kind; `throws :TooBigError |~~ A sum past a bound. ~~|`
a refusal and its page; and `impl ~(add by)` or `impl ::builtin{}` its
recipe. Every name a function holds is written as every name is, with
its doc and its value. `apply` runs a function on its subject, the
objects after it its arguments, `5 | apply f 3` answering `8`; `fill 3`
writes arguments into the head of its code,
`~(:by |~~ How much. ~~| 3 | add by)`; and `params` answers its inputs.
A function prints as its literal, its inputs and its answer as
declarations in a quote and its recipe after it,
`::fn[~(:by |~~ How much. ~~| /0) ~(add by)]`, which the constructor of
`::fn` reads back, and one with no inputs reads as its quote.

A word is a function named under a label, the chain written after its
name and its page:

```
:number/plus |~~ Adds by to the subject. ~~|
  ::fn
  | takes :by |~~ How much. ~~| /0 ::number
  | returns |~~ The subject and by added. ~~| ::number
  | impl ~(add by)
```

A body begun on a later line than its name is a sentence, its steps
joined by `|` up to the next declaration or a value that heads the
sentence after it, and a body on the line of its name is one step, as
[D66] reads a body's line apart. The inputs, the answer and the
refusals of a word are the definitions under its address, read from
the function it names, which `manifest` and `doc` answer, and the kind
of an input names its refusal, `::number/plus/by/NotNumberError`. A doc
is the object of a command as any value is. `fn.qlang` declares `::fn`
and the words that build it, and writes as their literal the words the
chain is built by, which it cannot build before they stand; every other
word, in the catalog and in a query, is written by the chain.
