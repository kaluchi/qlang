# D251 · A quote is data, a recipe reads what its call hands it, and a function is built from the noun `::fn` by commands

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The maintainer: a quote pure data, with no scope of its own
and no verb, a verb belonging to its subject and a quote taking part in
its recipe, which a host's primitive may be instead; a value a word's
recipe needs taken as a part or written in it; a function built by
commands from the noun `::fn`, `takes`, `returns`, `throws` and its
recipe last by `impl`, a word written so with no group around its chain;
every name with its doc and its value, an input's kind among what
`takes` takes, several kinds as a set; a function that holds its roles
itself, no adapter into the declarations a word was written as, and no
place that reads the rest of the args; `~(query /0 1)` as the place of an
input with a default; `fn.qlang` writing its own words as their
literal; and the catalog written by the chain, its lines broken in the
style it keeps. The model's, on the axes the maintainer left it: the literal as
the chain folded into data, the command `on` for the subject and `holds`
for an invariant, the call that reads a function's roles, `apply` handing
a function a vector of arguments, `fill` and `params`, the nodes a word
lays under its address, the scope a recipe, the code of its definition
and the code it runs read, the body of a definition begun on a later line
read as a sentence, a doc as the object of a command, the inputs a
host's primitive runs as code named where it is bound, the kinds of each
element, the name of a kind's refusal given by the kinds, and the
declaration form kept for a word a reader or a host's source writes.
Decision. A quote is data: its steps and nothing else, so two quotes
`eq` holds of answer alike wherever they run, and
`:f (:k 10 | :g ~(mul k) | g) | :k 2 | :h ~(mul k) | [(f | eq h) (3 | apply f) (3 | apply h)]`
answers `[true 6 6]`. A word is a node under the label of its subject,
and its recipe, a quote or a host's primitive, runs in the scope its
call opens: its inputs and `args`, the names its steps declare, and the
tree, the words and the nodes of the root and those the query declared
with a path or as a label. No value a sentence names reaches a recipe,
where the word was declared or where it is called, so
`:n 3 | :number/times ~(mul n) | 5 | times` is refused, and a value a
recipe needs is an input, with a default where a call may leave it out,
or is written in it. Nor does one reach the code of a word's
definition, the places its inputs read and its invariants, which run
where its recipe runs, a word of the host's among them:
`:it 5 | 3 | is ::number` answers `true`, and
`:floor 3 | [1 2 3] | drop 1` answers `[2 3]`. A quote written in the
step that runs it runs in the scope of that step:
`[1 2] | :k 10 | map ~(mul k)` answers `[10 20]`, and the step
`map ~(add by)` of a recipe reads the input `by`. Any other code a verb
runs, a quote held in a name or an input or read from a page, runs in
the tree of names of the query, the scope of the reader's step its call
began from, and no name a recipe writes, an input, `args`, a name its
steps declare, reaches it [D200]: a quote handed to a word written in
the language reads the names of its author, though an input of the word
bear one of them, and `:f (:k 10 | ~(mul k)) | 3 | apply f` is refused,
no step of its query having named `k`.

A function is a value of the kind `::fn`: the roles of its signature and
its recipe, code, a host's primitive, or none for a contract no call
runs. It is built from the noun by commands, its own words taking the
tag as their subject [D88], each written after the ones before it and
the recipe last: `on |~~ A number. ~~| ::number` names its subject, its
page and the kinds it serves; `takes :by |~~ How much. ~~| /0 ::number`
an input, its name, its page, the place of the call it reads, a path,
`/0` or `/0/by`, or a quote, `~(query /0 1)` the first object or one, and
its kinds, a tag or a set of tags, `#[::fn ::path ::quote]`;
`returns |~~ The sum. ~~| ::number` its answer, its page and its kinds;
`throws :TooBigError |~~ A sum past a bound. ~~|` a refusal and its page;
`holds :by/TooBigError |~~ Under a hundred. ~~| ~(lt 100)` an invariant
of an input or of the answer, the refusal of a value that breaks it, its
page and its condition; `law :addsBy |~~ Five and three is eight. ~~| ~(5 | plus 3 | eq 8)`
a law, the claim its name makes, its page and the query that proves it,
under a name the function declares where it is written with a path, the
refusal an input's kinds name among them; and `impl ~(add by)` or
`impl ::builtin{}` its recipe, last. A name of the signature is declared once, an input's of one
segment beside `answer` and `subject`, and a refusal or an invariant
written with a path is named under an input taken before it or under
the answer; every name is written with its page. A function prints as
its literal, the chain folded into data, its commands in a quote and its
recipe after it, `::fn[~(takes :by |~~ How much. ~~| /0) ~(add by)]`,
which the constructor of `::fn` reads back, and one of no commands is
its code, `::fn | impl ~(add 1)` answering `~(add 1)`.

The call of a function reads its roles from the function alone. The
subject is served by the walk of its tags to the kinds `on` names, and,
for a word, to its label or to them beside it, a tag for a word of the
noun itself. Each input reads its place from the args, a path by `get`,
a quote that reads one place by `query` without a call and any other
quote run over the args, and holds the value of its kind the walk of its
tags reaches; a value of none of its kinds is refused under the input,
`::number/plus/by/NotNumberError`, an input whose strict place the args
leave out under the input, `…/by/NotGivenError`, args past the places
the inputs read under the call, and a value that breaks an invariant
under the invariant's name, the invariant holding of the value as the
args give it. Kinds written as a vector of one tag or one set,
`[::number]`, are the kinds of each element: the input holds a vector,
or a set, of the value of its kind of each, an element of none refused
under `…/ElementNotNumberError` with its index, and a value that is no
vector under `…/NotVecError`. The name of the refusal kinds raise is
given by them, in the order of their set, `…/code/NotPathOrQuoteError`.
An object of the call that failed where its
input takes a quote is refused under the input's kinds with the error at
rest [D67], [D167]. The recipe runs with each input bound by its name
and the args as `args`, a primitive handed the inputs in the order
`takes` names them, and the answer is brought to the kinds `returns`
names and held to the invariants under it. A host's primitive names
where its implementation is bound the inputs it runs as code,
`runs(['code'], …)`, and its call hands it each as code closed at the
call, a quote written in the step in the scope of the step, a path read
as `get` reads it, and each element so of an input that holds the
objects of the call whole; a quote is handed to any other input as the
data it is. A word is a function named
under a label, its refusals named under its address; `apply` runs a
function on its subject with a vector of arguments, as a call holds its
objects, `5 | apply f [3]` answering `8`, its refusals named under the
call of a function, `::fn/call/by/NotNumberError`, and a quote and a
path take no arguments. `fill 3` writes the arguments of a call into the
head of the code, each input a declaration with its page of the value it
reads, as its call reads it, `~(:by |~~ How much. ~~| 3 | add by)`, and
`params` answers the inputs, a map of its name, its page, its place and
its kinds for each.

A word is written as the chain after its name and its page:

```
:number/plus |~~ Adds by to the subject. ~~|
  ::fn
  | takes :by |~~ How much. ~~| /0 ::number
  | returns |~~ The subject and by added. ~~| ::number
  | impl ~(add by)
```

The body of a definition, a name with a path, begun on a later line
than its name is a sentence, its steps joined by `|` up to the next
declaration or a value that heads the sentence after it, and a body on
the line of its name is one step, as [D66] reads a body's line apart;
the body of a bare name is one step wherever it begins. A doc is the
object of a command as any value is. A function named under a label
lays its roles under its address as nodes, its subject, each input with
its place, the refusal its kinds name with the page `throws` gives it or
one that names its kinds, its answer, each refusal `throws` pages and
each invariant, which `manifest`, `doc` and the tools read; its call
reads none of them, and a node a reader declares under it changes no
call. `fn.qlang` declares `::fn` and the words that build, fill and read
it, written as their literal, which it cannot build before they stand,
and loads after the topic and `builtin`, its laws in them; every other word of the
catalog, the command line's among them, is written by the chain, each
command led by its pipe under the `f` of `::fn`, as the lines of a law
stand under the `l` of `::law`, a long page in a block under its
command and the objects after it on a line of their own, as a
declaration's body stands under its page, and a long recipe a step a
line, its pipes two columns into its quote, and the laws of a word stand
in its chain before its recipe, so a source declares its words and no
law of one beside them. A later line that opens a declaration ends the
modifiers of a command before it as [D243] reads it, its keyword and
its doc or the step its body opens with read and no more, so each
declaration of a source is read once; inside a quote of a doc the
declaration is read whole. A word a
reader or a host's source writes as declarations under its address is
called by them as before. A quote of several steps one of which takes
several lines prints a step a line, each after the first led by its
pipe, as a quote of declarations prints.
