# D269 · Open: the rule of collisions of a host's names

Domain. Language.
Question. Names collide, which the maintainer has named as the one
worry the design never resolved: «стремительно растущий зоопарк
операндов меня все время беспокоил и конфликты имен .. последнее так и
осталось неразрешенным до сих пор» (maintainer, 2026-09-23 07:02,
session 86982eb5). The surface and the loading [D153], the reading of a
name [D221], the rule of the owner [D200] and the meeting of a value and
a word [D223] are settled, and a host still publishes names without a
prefix: the sister project's names would meet the core's on `type` and
on `source`, which it names `typeNamed` and `sourceText` until it
declares them under kinds of its own. What remains is who may join a
verb and a kind, whose the names without a prefix are, and how one query
shows every definition of a name and which one wins, which two queries
show, a subject reading the word a name reaches for it by
`binding`, and the refusal of a subject no word of the name serves
listing the addresses where its words live:

```qlang
> [1 2] | binding :count | get /name
:vec/count

> 5 | binding :count !| get /addresses
#[::map/count ::set/count ::string/count ::vec/count]
```
Source. The scar of names in one root, where the audit held these
intentions from its first version, and the maintainer's words the
question quotes.
Alternatives. A verb and a kind joined by whoever owns one of them, the
orphan rule of Rust: a host specializes a core verb on its own tags and
declares its own verbs, but redefines no core verb on the core's kinds,
so `source` on the sister project's node tags resolves by the subject,
while its `type`, the reader of identity every error handler and sort
relies on, moves into a field or a tag. Names without a prefix the
core's, as EDN reserves its unprefixed tags, since a shared space hosts
write into freezes the core out of its own names, as
`Array.prototype.flatten` could not ship in JavaScript because a library
had put another `flatten` there. One query that shows every definition
of a name and which one wins, as `type -a` does in bash, beside the two
that show it.
