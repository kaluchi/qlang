# D278 · Open: the names a quote held as data reads

Domain. Language.
Question. A quote held as data, in a name, an input or a page, runs in
the tree of names of the query, the scope of the reader's step its call
began from, and no name a recipe writes reaches it [D251]: its free names
are read where it runs, not where it was written. A name the reader
declares in a step around the call is read before the one the quote's
author saw, and a name declared after the quote is read too:

```qlang
> [(:k 1 | :f ~(add k) | [5] | map ~(:k 100 | apply f)) (:f ~(add k) | :k 10 | 1 | apply f)]
[[105] 11]
```

The satisfactory state names lexical scope among the concepts of the
core. The outside audit before found the opposite trap, a quote written
as the body of a declaration closing over a scope its print did not
show, which [D251] took out: a quote is the data its print shows, and
two quotes `eq` holds equal run alike.
Source. The outside audit at cbaaeb2d, its first problem and its first
disagreement with the project's audit,
`docs/research/outside-audit/2026-10-13-cbaaeb2d.md`, and the one at
3495de5d, `docs/research/outside-audit/2026-10-13-3495de5d.md`.
Alternatives. The names read where the quote runs, as they are, which
keeps a quote the data its print shows and lets the steps around a call
shadow the names its author saw; the names bound where the quote is
declared, written into its head as declarations, `:f ~(add k)` holding
`~(:k 1 | add k)` as `fill` writes the arguments of a call, which its
print shows and `eq` compares, at the cost of a declaration that changes
the value it is given; or a quote that reads a name its scope does not
hold refused where it is declared, at the cost of a quote written before
the names it reads.
