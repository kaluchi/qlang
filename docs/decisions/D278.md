# D278 · Open: the names a quote held as data reads

Domain. Language.
Question. A quote held as data, in a name, an input or a page, runs in
the tree of names of the query, the scope of the reader's step its call
began from, and no name a recipe writes reaches it [D251]: its free names
are read where it runs, not where it was written. A name the reader
declares in a step around the call is read before the one the quote's
author saw, and a name declared after the quote is read too:

```qlang
> get [~(:k 1 | :f ~(add k) | [5] | map ~(:k 100 | apply f)) ~(:f ~(add k) | :k 10 | 1 | apply f)]
[[105] 11]
```

The satisfactory state names lexical scope among the concepts of the
core. The outside audit before found the opposite trap, a quote written
as the body of a declaration closing over a scope its print did not
show, which [D251] took out: a quote is the data its print shows, and
two quotes `eq` holds equal run alike.

Alternatives. The names read where the quote runs, as they are, the rule
told on the pages of a quote and of `apply` with its laws: a quote is
the data its print shows, a step around a call may shadow a name its
author saw, and a value a reader fixes into code is written there as
the input of a function, `apply f [1]`, or by `fill`; or a quote that
reads a name its scope does not hold refused where it is declared, at
the cost of a quote written before the names it reads, which leaves the
shadow by a step around the call as it is; or code held as data run in
a lane of its own, as the call of a function runs, its past the subject
it runs on, a function's inputs beside it, and the words of the tree,
no value a reader names, a quote written in a step reading the names of
that step [D279]: a held quote is then read from its text alone, as a
literal is, and a held quote and a function of no commands [D253] run
by one rule, at the cost, measured on the suite in the session, of the
cases that show the rule of [D251], `:big ~(gt lim) | :lim 4 | [1 5 10] | filter big`
refused where it answers `[5 10]`, of a recursion by a quote in a name,
which turns into a word, and of the laws and snippets `runLaws` and
`open` run, which read the words alone.
Set aside. The names bound where the quote is declared, written into
its head as declarations, `:f ~(add k)` holding `~(:k 1 | add k)` as
`fill` writes the arguments of a call: a declaration that turns the
quote it is given into another value is the hidden rewrite the
maintainer refused.
