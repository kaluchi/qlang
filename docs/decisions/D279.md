# D279 · A literal is closed from its opening to its closing pair: it reads neither the scope nor the value in the pipe, and a constructor attaches its tag or refuses

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The maintainer: a literal a form that depends on neither the
scope nor the value in the pipe; its opening the strongest sign of it,
so a reader who sees `/`, `[`, `{`, `#`, `~`, `::`, `:` or `|~~` knows
that up to the closing pair everything inside is literal and nothing
else; the `::` a literal opens with, written or implied, closing every
way to the value in the pipe and to the scope; a constructor that
attaches its tag to the payload or refuses it, saying why it cannot,
nothing inside its payload computed; the old active projection and the
reshaping as what the reading of the language kept against it; and the
payload of a tagged literal touching its tag by a sign that continues
the literal, `[`, `{`, `"` or `#`, never a parenthesis, a number, a
boolean, null and a path taking `#`, `::Width#5`, `::Pos#-3`, `::T#true`, `::T#/a`; a shape computed
by `get` over a template of paths and quotes, which reads the paths and
runs the quotes against its subject; and the recipe of a kind gone but
the host's, `impl ::builtin{}`, read as a base check that attaches or
refuses; and a set that orders its payload by the one order, the
literal `#[3 1 3]` the set `#[1 3]`. The model's: the literals that already touch a tag by a sigil
of their own kept as they are, a quote, a doc, a keyword, an error and a
tag; a group or a name inside a container refused, naming `get`; the
other values of a template answered as they are; the `#` read as the
sign a literal takes after a tag where its first character would
continue the tag's name, not as a joint, on the maintainer's question of
one joint for every payload; and that rule said of the literal and its
first character, on his question of the terms.
Decision. A literal is a value its text alone fixes. From its opening
sigil to its closing pair nothing inside it reads the scope or the
value in the pipe: no group runs there, no name is read there, and no
part of it is computed against a subject. Every literal opens with the
`::` of its kind, written, `::Box{…}`, or implied by its brackets,
`{…}` the literal of `::map`, `[…]` of `::vec`, `#[…]` of `::set`,
`!{…}` of `::error`, `~(…)` of `::quote`, `|~~ … ~~|` of `::doc`, `/a` of
`::path`, `:k` of `::keyword` [D20]. The constructor of the kind attaches
its tag to the payload the literal writes or refuses it, naming why the
payload does not fit, a payload broken by a hand that wrote it; it
builds no other value from it. What computes is a verb that takes a
literal: `get` reads a path, `apply` runs a quote [D248].

A tagged literal writes its payload touching the tag by a sign that
continues the literal: a vector `::T[…]`, a map `::T{…}`, a set
`::T#[…]`, a string `::T"…"`, a pattern `::T#"…"`, and the literals that
open with a sigil of their own, a quote `::T~(…)`, a doc
`::T|~~ … ~~|`, a keyword `::T:k`, an error `::T!{…}`, a tag `::A::B[…]`.
A literal whose first character would continue the tag's name, a
number, `true`, `false`, `null` or a path, takes `#`: `::Width#5`,
`::Pos#-3`, `::T#true`, `::T#/a`, and prints so, since `::T5`, `::T-3`,
`::Ttrue` and `::T/a` each read as the name of one tag. The `#` is the
sign of such a literal there, as `#[` opens a set and `#"` a pattern, so
a tag touches the sign its payload opens with and no joint stands
between them; a string and a keyword, atoms as a number is [D42], touch
it by their own signs. Parentheses after a tag, the sign of a group and of code,
leave the tagged literal, and a value computed goes under a tag by the
verb `tag`, `1 | add 2 | tag ::Box`.

A container holds literals alone: a group or a name written inside
`[…]`, `{…}`, `#[…]` or `!{…}` is refused by the parser, which names the
verb that computes a shape. That verb is `get`: its template is a
literal of paths and quotes at any depth of its vectors, maps and sets,
each path read and each quote run against the subject, any other value
of it answered as it is,
`{:first "Ann" :age 30} | get {:who /first :next ~(get /age | add 1)}`
answering `{:who "Ann" :next 31}`, and `query` reads its paths as
softly, `{:a 1} | query [~(get /a) /b]` answering `[1 null]`. A
container under a tag is read as well and laid back under its tags
through their constructors, which check the template as its literal
writes it and then the payload read, as a write of a path lays it
[D250], so a kind whose field takes a number refuses a path written at
that field and such a value is built by `get` and then `tag`,
`{:x 3} | get {:w /x} | tag ::Box`; and a quote, a doc, a function and a kind are
answered as they are. A quote
written in the template of a step reads the names of the step's scope,
as a quote written as a modifier does, and a template held as data, in
a name or a page, runs its quotes in a lane of its own [D278]. A kind
attaches its tag by its commands, `wraps`, `reads` and
`holds`, and a kind of the core by the check the host hands,
`impl ::builtin{}`; a recipe that builds another value from the payload
leaves the chain of a kind. A set orders the payload its literal writes
by the one order and holds each element once [D16], a function of the
literal alone, which no scope and no subject touch: `#[3 1 3]` is
`#[1 3]`, since a reader may not know the order.
