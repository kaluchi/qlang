# D46 · A refusal keeps the tag of its site, declared by the address of the place it guards, its facts definitions under it, and says what the reader meant

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The maintainer, on the readings the model laid out to him;
the addresses, the places, the facts and the constants of the refusals
are the model's, at his word to go through them.
Decision. Every place that refuses has a tag of its own, its address,
named so that a reader knows at once what failed and pointing at that
one place in the system: the address of the place it guards and its
last name, `::number/add/SubjectNotNumberError` for the subject of `add`
and `::number/add/addend/NotNumberError` for its part. The last name
does not say the place again, `::keyword/PayloadNotKeywordError`, so two
places may share one, `::call/ReadBackDiffersError` beside
`::proj/ReadBackDiffersError`, and it says in the language's words what
was refused and what the reader met rather than how the runtime met it,
`::construct/TagNotKindError`, `::tagged/payload/SubjectNotTaggedError`,
`::call/KindAnswersNoVerbError` for `5 | count` and
`::call/UnknownNameError`. The refusal is declared once, in the catalog
under the address of what it guards, by its page, which is the site's
procedure [D7]; a site passes the facts and the declarations under its
tag check them, so no place adds or drops a field another fills.

A refusal of the kernel sits under the kind of the place it guards, in
the source of that kind, and its page links the page of that place: the
refusals of a projection under `::proj`, of a constructor under a tag
under `::construct`, of a declaration under `::bind`, of a call under
`::call`, of reading text as code under `::quote`, of the path of an
error under `::error`, and of a codec under the verbs of that codec.
What no reader reaches by a query, the table of primitives, the schema
of a session, a node of the tree the evaluator does not know, a value
the printer cannot write, is an invariant of the implementation and sits
under `::builtin`, the kind of the kernel, declared in `builtin.qlang`
with its refusals and those invariants, the table of implementations's
`::builtin/ImplementationNotHandedError` among them. The refusal of a
primitive takes the address of the word that ran it,
`::vec/sum/ElementNotNumberError` for the sum of a vector and
`::set/sum/ElementNotNumberError` for a set's, and the refusal of the
construct of a kind stays under its label.

A fact a refusal carries is a definition of the catalog: a name, its
page and the kind it holds, as data under the label of the refusal,
`:proj/IndexOutOfBoundsError/length ::number`, and under `::error` for
the facts the refusals share, the index of an element, the kind of the
value refused, the places of the args and the value itself. A fact is
no word: it is read by projection, `!| /actualType`. An error holds its
facts in the order they are declared, the label's own before those of
`::error` and the trail last [D13], so the signal comes in the first
characters and elision takes the tail; a fact the runtime raises that
neither declares is a defect of the runtime, so the catalog is the one
spelling of what an error carries. A fact holds a value of the language,
a name a keyword, an address a tag, a key a keyword and an index a
number; the name a step read is `:name`, and a refusal over a map names
the entry by its `:key`. A failure of the host carries the facts every
host can give, its account and the accounts of the failures beneath it,
the step that failed standing in the trail; the stack, the class and the
fields of the host's error stay with the host, which declares a refusal
of its own where it has facts to hand.

A refusal says what a reader meant. One that meets a form a reader
writes by habit names the form that is right: a pipe written after a
combinator, `[1] !| | count`, is refused by the parser with the
sentence that `|` stands in the place of the combinator, and a set
written with braces, `#{1}`, with the sentence that a set is written
`#[…]`. A name no word answers is refused with the addresses where its
words live: a tag whose path names no word, `::any/links`, among them
`::doc/links`; the refusals of the axes, `doc`, `source`, `binding`,
`explain`, `laws` and `runLaws`, each under a tag of its own,
`::any/doc/UnknownNameError` among them, `::count | doc !| /addresses`
answering the words of `count` on maps, sets, strings and vectors; and a
refusal of a call alike, `5 | count !| /addresses`.

A refusal is declared in the code by its address as a literal,
`'noun/LastNameError'`, which the factory of its site takes, the site's
spec recorded under it and the tag of the error value being it; the
constant that holds its class is named by the address without its slash,
each name capitalised, `map/ElementNotPairError` held by
`MapElementNotPairError` and `call/UnknownNameError` by
`CallUnknownNameError`, so a grep for the address finds the declaration,
the page and every case, a grep for the constant the declaration and
every throw, and neither name is a part of the other. A JavaScript class
answers its last name as `name` and its address as `address`, and no
process-wide map from a last name to an address stands.
`check:conventions` refuses a refusal not declared by such a literal and
a constant the address does not give.
