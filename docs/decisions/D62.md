# D62 · The core is the noun `::qlang`, and every walk ends at `::qlang/any`

Domain. Language.
Decision. The core is a noun, `::qlang`, and every noun of the core is
named under it and prints short [D32]. The page of `::qlang` is the
root doc a fresh session reads first [D27], and its namespace holds the
core's nouns, beside which a session mounts the nouns of its hosts
[D35], [D36]: `::qlang | manifest` lists them, and the command line,
whose pipe starts from `::qlang` [D37], answers a bare `manifest` alike.
A verb resolves against the subject by walking its tags down to the
core's kind [D34], and beneath every kind lies one more, `::qlang/any`,
where every walk ends and the verbs of any value live: `type`, `eq`,
`apply`, `if`, `cond`, `coalesce`, `json`, `env`, and the reading of a
value by its kind that `docs`, `spec`, `source` and `examples` answer
[D61]. A verb whose head names no subject kind lives there [D45]. The
two kinds of name look in two places. A keyword names a binding of the
scope where it stands, the declarations of its query or its module, and
a tag name is an address in the tree of names from its root [D36]:
`::qlang/vec` a noun, `::qlang/vec/count` the verb `count` on vectors,
`::qlang/count` the contract of `count` the core owns [D34], and
`::jdt/Method/callers` a verb a host keeps on its kind; printed short,
the core's addresses read `::vec/count` and `::count` [D32]. The same
four verbs are implemented on `::qlang/keyword` by the binding of the
scope, so `:count | docs` answers where `count` is declared and is
refused elsewhere with the addresses where the name lives, and on
`::qlang/tag` by the node the address names, so `::count | docs` reads
the page of `count`; `manifest` is a verb of `::qlang/tag`, asked of a
namespace by its noun, `::jdt | manifest`. A
noun's own verbs are names of its namespace, which the noun opens in
the subject position [D35], `::jdt | projects`. `type` never answers
`::qlang/any`, since every value has a kind of its own above it; the
verbs of a kind are its own, `::qlang/string | spec | /verbs` listing
the verbs of strings, and the verbs of any value are one step further
down, `::qlang/any | spec | /verbs`. A step spells a verb sent to the
subject and a value read from the scope alike, a bare name, and a name
resolves nearest first: the declarations of the scope where it stands,
then the namespace the subject's noun opened [D35], then the walk of the
subject's tags down to `::qlang/any`. The core's names come last, so a
verb the core or a host adds later never changes the meaning of a query
written before it, and a user's declaration wins within its scope over
every verb in view [D23]: `:count 5 | [1 2 3] | count` answers `5`, as
the user's own text shows, and the verb it shadows stays one address
away, `::vec/count | docs`. A bare name with a path calls by address,
from the root and without a search [D35]: `:count 5 | [1 2 3] |
vec/count` answers `3`, and `[1 2 3] | vec/filter ~(gt 1)` stands where
`filter ~(gt 1)` stands, so a shadowed verb, one of two that open
namespaces both know, or one found as data is called by the address it
was read by; the bare name carries a path as the keyword and the tag
name do, and the sentence keeps its shape, `S | V O`, with the verb's
own head behind the call. One query shows every definition
of a name and which one wins [D23]. A host publishes no name without a
prefix, which belong to the core, and puts its verbs on its own tags
[D34], so the core and its hosts grow without taking a name from each
other.

Set aside. The verbs of any value copied onto each kind of the core,
which buries the few verbs of a kind under the many of every value. A
fallback from the walk into the core's namespace, a second mechanism for
one verb where D35 lets the search happen only where a name enters the
text. The underscore, `::qlang/_`, the wildcard of patterns, which a
reader learns from a page where `any` says what it is. A refusal of
every declaration that shares a name with a verb in view, which takes
the name from every query written before the core or a host grows by
it. The core's names first, the order of D35, under which a verb the
core adds changes the meaning of every query that declared its name. A
mark that tells a verb sent from a value read, a token of its own where
the nearest declaration already decides. `env` as a verb of the core's
noun: it answers the scope where it stands, whatever the subject. A
keyword that names a verb wherever the verb is declared, the reading of
the flat catalog, under which `:count` named the one `count` a single
environment held. An address written as a keyword with a path,
`:qlang/vec/count`, which reads a local name as a global one. A verb
that sends by address, `send ::vec/filter ~(gt 1)` as Ruby's `send` and
Smalltalk's `perform:` do, which puts the verb in the place of an
object and gives the call a second spelling. `apply` over the value of a
verb, the overloading by the kind of its argument D9 sets aside. A tag
name in the place of a step that calls, where `| ::jdt` has to answer
the noun.
Replaces in part [D34], under which the walk ends at the core's kind and
the global verbs of the first formulation are the verbs of the core's
kinds.
Replaces in part [D35], under which a bare name resolves first among the
core's names.
Replaces in part [D61], under which a keyword reads the binding it
