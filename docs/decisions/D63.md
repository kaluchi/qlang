# D63 · A module is a pipeline, and its value is what it exposes

Domain. Language.
Decision. A declaration hands its subject on unchanged, the binding
being a branch to the side, and writes into the scope a record,
`::qlang/binding`, that holds the name, the doc of the binding's slot,
the value, the declaration's source and the module it came from [D5];
`env` answers the records of the scope where it stands [D36], so `5 |
:x |~~ note ~~| 1 | add 1` answers `6` and `5 | :x |~~ note ~~| 1 | env
| /x` the record of `x`. A module is a pipeline its provider runs once,
from the provider's noun in the subject position [D35], which its
declarations hand on, so its subject changes only at the step that
exposes, and its value is what it exposes: `env` as the last step
exposes every name the module declared; a part of it, `env | inter
#[:split :lines]`, keeps the rest in the module's scope, where the verbs
that use it still see it [D44]; and a module of data ends in its map.
The loader keeps that value under the module's name, and nothing merges
into another scope: `use` binds the entries of a namespace in the scope
where it stands, a record as the binding it is and any other value as a
binding without a doc, so the parsed sources, the export maps and the
host's locator leave the scope, and a module that loads another passes
on only what its own value holds. A namespace is reached by its noun,
`::qlang | spec | /split` answering the record of `split`. Inside it a
declaration points at another by its name: the kind of a slot at its
kind [D60], a refusal at the verb it guards [D46], a verb at the kind it
lives on through its head [D45]. The metadata of a verb is therefore
the set of declarations that name it, reached by a projection or a
filter over the namespace. The axes are projections of the record,
`source` its source, `docs` its doc, `examples` the quotes among the
doc's segments [D19] and `spec` the declaration of its value, which
`::qlang/binding` implements, so `env | /x | docs` answers the doc of
`x`.

A declaration runs nothing: a verb literal, a doc and a tag's
declaration are literals, and a verb's body runs only when the verb is
called. A module of the core therefore declares with literals alone and
loads from the kernel, which is the evaluator with its primitives, the
shape `::builtin` and the two verbs of its state, `env` and `use`, all
of which the kernel defines and the catalog documents [D36]. The modules of
the core need no order among themselves: a verb's body resolves the
core's names through `::qlang` when it runs, after the core stands. What
a namespace derives, the verbs of a kind, the refusals of a verb, the
nouns a session reaches, is computed in qlang when it is asked. A module
above the core, a host's or a project's, may compute while it loads,
with the core beneath it. A module that needs another's verbs names it
with `use`, the loader bringing that module in first, and a module that
reaches itself through its own `use` is refused by name as a cycle.

Set aside. The difference of the environment, which exports every
helper and every name the module's own `use` brought in. A keyword of
export or a convention of an underscore, a second mechanism beside the
value. A module's value taken from its scope when it ends in a
declaration, a second rule where `env` says the same in one word. The
map literal of the whole catalog, order-free because it is one literal,
which loses the steps, the highlighting and the doc of each
declaration. An operand that declares, seeded before the catalog and
redeclared at its tail. Computation while the core loads, which brings
the order back. A scope of the kernel that binds every primitive under
its name, which gives each name two bindings and runs calls that no
declaration checks. The resolver of module directories, a second loader
beside the one D36 keeps. A cycle found by the budget of depth, which
names the symptom where the loader knows the module.
