# D66 · A declaration's body may be a keyword or a tag name

Domain. Language.
Decision. The body of a declaration may be a keyword or a tag name, so
`:s ::vec` names the kind `::vec` and `:status :active` names the
keyword, where the grammar refuses both today and reads a keyword or a
tag name after a name as the start of the next declaration. A
declaration that names a kind is written as every other one, its name,
its doc and the kind in the place of the body. A word after such a body
is refused, so `:s ::vec /` no longer reads as the keyword `:s`
followed by a declaration of `::vec` in the scope.
Source. «да, наверное я тоже за первое...» (maintainer, 2026-09-25
19:10, session 86982eb5), choosing the first of the model's two
alternatives after «а почему qlang> :s ::vec / - работает а qlang> :s
::vec нет?» (18:57), which the model traced to `BindBody` in
`core/src/grammar.peggy`, refusing a keyword or a tag name as a body
since `3380181`, and to the pipe a declaration written after a space
stands for since `ceb3b59` [D55].
Set aside. Keeping the refusal and writing the kind in parentheses,
`:key (::quote)`, which today's binding reads as a conduit, `:s (::vec) |
env | /s | /value` answering `::conduit[:s [] ~((::vec))]`, and which
leaves `:s ::vec /` declaring `::vec` in the scope without a word. The
price accepted: a chain of declarations whose next one starts with a
keyword, `:x :y 2`, reads the keyword as the body of the first.
