Decision. A law is a definition under the address whose behaviour it
proves: its name says the claim, its page is the sentence of the claim,
and its value is `::law~(…)`, a query that answers true,
`:vec/take/takesNothingByANegativeCount |~~ A negative count takes
nothing. ~~| ::law~([1 2 3] | take -1 | eq [])`. It stands under the
address beside its inputs, its answer and its refusals, the nodes the
chain of its word lays [D251]. Every word that reads a law names it
a law, and none reads it under a second name: `laws` answers the laws
under the name a value reads, as `doc` reads it, each by its page and
its query, in the order of the declarations, so a reader meets the
claims and their code in one answer; `runLaws` runs the laws of a name
as held code runs [D278], and answers for each law
`{:law :says :actual :ok :error}`: `:law` its address, `:says` its page,
the claim it proves, as `laws` answers it, null for a law without one,
and `:error` the error its query answered at rest, as `!|` grounds it, a
value whose tag names the refusal, `get /error | type` answering
`::number/add/SubjectNotNumberError`, null where the law answered a
value, neither flattened to a string, so a failure reads as the claim
that broke. `manifest` lists the anatomy of a name, its inputs, its
answer, its refusals and the names under it, and leaves its laws to
`laws`, `::vec/take | manifest | has ::vec/take/takesNothingByANegativeCount`
answering `false`. A page holds the prose about its name [D168] and
says two things, each once: its prose tells what the name is for and how
it relates to the rest, with its links, and its claims tell how it
behaves, each a law; a behaviour the prose tells and a claim proves is
told by the claim alone, and a sentence that states the rule a law is an
instance of stays prose in the page, the chapters of `::qlang` among
them.
