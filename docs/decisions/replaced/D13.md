# D13 · An error rests as a value and flies as a failure the pipe alone carries: `raise` raises it, `!|` alone catches it, and its trail is the path it flew

Left at 95a0454d, the tree this text was last true of: a path, a case or a
probe it names reads there, `git show 95a0454d:<path>`, not in today's.

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The maintainer, on the readings the model laid out to him;
`coalesce`, the empty path of a failure that flew no step and the stop
of a catch are the model's.
Decision. An error has two states. At rest it is a value like any other,
a map of facts under `::error`, under the tag of the site that refused,
`::number/add/SubjectNotNumberError::error{…}`: a container holds it, a
step reads it, `print` writes it, and its print reads back as itself.
Raised, it is a failure, which lives in the pipe alone: `|` passes it
by, and `!|` alone catches it, its step running on the error at rest, a
value from then on, `x !| trail | last` and `x !| explain`, which
`raise` raises again, resuming the path it holds, `x !| raise` being
`x`.

The kind of errors is `::error`, the walk of an error passing the tag of
its site, then `::error`, then the map [D34], so a word declared once on
`::error` reaches every error, and a path reads the map beneath
the tags, `x !| get /actualType`, `::Box::Crate{:a 1} | get /a` answering `1`.
An error literal is the literal of the kind, as `#[…]` is the set's:
`!{…}` is `::error{…}`, and `::Foo!{…}` is `::Foo::error{…}`, the facts
laid under the tag written before the bang through its label, a refusal
of the label answering for the literal; the printer writes an error at
rest with its bang, `[!{:a 1}] | get /0` answering `!{:a 1}`. A `:kind`
entry names no tag: it is a fact of the error as any key is,
`!{:kind ::Foo} | get [~(type) /kind]` answering `[::error ::Foo]`. The
highlighter paints the bang of an error literal in the colours of the
fail track, and `raise` as a word.

Raising is an act, and `raise` alone performs it: it puts its subject on
the fail track, a map as `::error{…}` and a value under a tag as the
error of the tag it shows, the outermost the walk passed, then the one
the map carries, then the kind of errors, `::Foo{:a 1} | raise` an error
of `::Foo`. A host makes an error of a tag by `makeErrorValue`.

No value holds a failure. A template of `get` whose quote answers one
answers it, the first in the order written, the quotes after it unrun,
`get [1 ~(1 | add "x") ~(2 | add "y")]` answering the refusal of `"x"`,
and so does the body of a declaration, which binds nothing; no tag
stands over a failure, so a literal whose part a constructor refuses
answers the refusal, `#[1 ::Pos#-3]` the refusal of `::Pos` where an
invariant holds its payload above zero, and `tag` hands on a failure in
its pipe as it is [D279]. A refusal that holds a failure computed at
the call
holds the error at rest, and an explanation the error at rest `!|`
caught, so a read never raises. The step of an error literal, which a
quote holds as data, is the error at rest it spells, under the tag
written before its bang, and the quote prints it with its bang, so a
quote is built back from its steps and the ring of atoms closes [D42].

The failure of a fork is handed to whatever ran the fork, which answers
it. A modifier of a step that answers a failure answers the call,
whatever the part it fills, and a key that answers a failure answers the
word that runs it, `sort` as `groupBy` and `indexBy` do, so a key that
misses its field is refused where it is read; `sort` without a key ranks
errors at rest in the one order [D16]. `coalesce` runs its alternatives
in order and takes an alternative that answers a failure as no value,
as it takes null, `1 | coalesce ~(add "x") 5` answering `5`.

A raised error holds the facts of its site and, after them, the path it
flew under `:trail`, `"x" | add 1 !| keys` answering
`#[:actualType :trail]`: a vector of stops from the step where it arose
to the step where it is read, a stop `{:step :subject :skipped}`, the
step that raised the error or handed it on, the subject that step had,
and the steps of its pipeline the error skipped after it. A step whose
answer is a failure its subject was not adds a stop: a step that fails,
`raise`, and a step that answers a failure from inside it, the call of a
word, a group, a word running a quote; a step the failure skips joins
the `:skipped` of the last stop. `/trail/0` is where the error arose and
`/trail/-1` the stop of the level that reads it, and `/trail/-1/skipped`
replays there, so every level has its own subject and its own skipped
steps, and a failure no step handles carries the path of the whole
computation. An error at rest holds the facts it is written with,
`::error{:a 1}` no `:trail`; an error or a map that writes its `:trail`,
an empty one among them, resumes that path when raised and adds no stop,
and the error at rest `!|` catches from an empty path holds none,
`{:a 1 :trail []} | raise !| keys` answering `#[:a]`, so an error printed
and read back, or built back from its atoms, is the error it was. The
word `trail` answers the path, `[]` for an error that holds none, while
`/trail` reads the key and is refused where there is none. A catch
whose label refuses the facts answers the refusal with a stop at its
step.

A failure prints as its error at rest, the literal `!|` grounds it to,
`::path/KeyNotInMapError!{…}`, the path it flew among its facts,
`:trail []` for one that flew no step, a query that does not parse among
them, which `raise` resumes, so its print under `| raise` is the failure
again. The answer of a host that is a failure, which a host meets alone
since a step skips it, prints under a line comment that says so,
`|~| raised, and no !| caught it`, which a reading of the literal passes
over: `printAnswer` writes the line, so every host prints it, and
`printValue` prints values alone, an error at rest an answer gives
printing under no line.
