elements and order, conditions, text, failures, asking, a worked task,

argument model is one rule: every modifier is computed once, at the
call, against the subject of the step, and a quote computes to itself,
so a word receives code unrun and runs it where it needs; `map` runs its
code on each element beside `filter`, `sort`, `groupBy` and `reduce`.
The rule of modifiers shows its trap beside its form: the subject of a
step is what the step before it answered, so in
`{:price 4 :qty 3} | get /price | mul (get /qty)` the `/qty` is read from
the price, a number with no parts, and the call is refused,
`::path/SubjectHasNoPartsError`; two values of one record meet in one
step, `mulOf /price /qty`, a law of the page holding it. Code is written
in its base form alone, `~(…)`, never `~name`. A rule stands in the
section it rules, not in a list of invariants ahead of the words it
speaks of; the words of a kind are found by `manifest`, not listed, and
the section on asking shows `map` over kinds,
`[::vec ::map ::string ::number ::any] | map ~(get [~(it) ~(manifest)])`,
its snippet, which answers each of those kinds beside its words in one
query, the kinds those whose words the children of its measurements
asked one call each, and the words of any value; the page shows the way
to ask and not the words, which stay where `manifest` reads them. A name
is read back by its bare word, its keyword staying a keyword; and
`toString` and `toKeyword` turn a keyword and a string into each other
[D244]. Kinds of one's own, invariants, args, the links and snippets of
a page, comments and the lines of a query joined at the top level are
