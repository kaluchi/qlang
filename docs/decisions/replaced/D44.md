# D44 · A binding names a value, and a word runs when it is mentioned

Domain. Language.
Decision. A binding names a value: its body is evaluated once, at
declaration, against the current value, and a quote body is a quote held
as a value, so `:q ~(add 1) | q` answers `~(add 1)`. A word is the same
form under the label of its subject, a quote its body,
`:number/inc ~(add 1)`; it runs when it is mentioned, as a built-in
does, and `~inc` hands it on as code. A value never runs by itself, and
`apply` runs a quote; a part that takes code applies what it receives,
so a named quote goes into it bare,
`:adult ~(/age | gte 18) | [{:age 30} {:age 12}] | filter adult`. A
quote written as a binding's body carries the scope of its declaration,
as one written as a modifier carries that of its call [D43]. A name is
declared once in a scope, and a second declaration there is refused; a
cell of the REPL opens a scope of its own. Under these rules code moved
into a declaration further left answers as it did inline or is refused:
a quote or a word means the same wherever its names mean the same, and
only a value, computed where it is declared, follows the subject there,
as its spelling shows.
