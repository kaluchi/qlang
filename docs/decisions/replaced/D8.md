Decision. A quote is code as a value: a vector of steps under
`::quote`, spelled `~(…)`, `~(add 1) | type` answering `::quote`; the
words of a vector apply to it as to any vector under a tag, its steps
values under the tags of their forms with no positions, while the
parser's tree stays a separate view for tools. A step is stored as the
record of its form, by the rule of a literal's elements: a call as
`::call`, its name and its modifiers, `~(add 1) | first` answering
`::call{:name :add :modifiers [1]}`; a declaration as `::bind`, `~(:q ~(add 1)) | first` answering
`::bind{:name :q :body ~(add 1)}`; a constructor invocation
`::T<payload>` as `::construct`, `~(::Box[1 2]) | first` answering
`::construct{:tag ::Box :payload [1 2]}`; a step of the fail track
under the wrapper `::fail`, `~(add 1 !| 0) | get /1` answering
`::fail~(0)`, and a group as its quote under `::group`; an error
literal as the error at rest it writes [D13]; and any other literal as
itself, a container holding the steps of its elements,
`~([1 ::Box[2]]) | first` answering `[1 ::construct{:tag ::Box :payload [2]}]`. A command's
modifiers are words [D10], each stored as its step, so `add ~(x)` and
`add x` stay apart, `[~(x)]` against `[::call{:name :x}]`, no quote is
doubled, `filter ~(gt 1)` holding `[~(gt 1)]`, and
`take (count | sub 1)` holds `[::group~(count | sub 1)]`: a field that
holds a word holds its step, one that holds a pipeline a quote, and
assembling a quote is writing what one reads, `5 | apply
([::call{:name :add :modifiers [1]}] | tag ::quote)` answering `6`. The
data form speaks with the five tags of the core, the records `::call`,
`::bind` and `::construct` and the wrappers `::fail` and `::group`, each with a source of its own and the refusals of its step
[D114]; `::tagged` keeps the page of the kind of tagged values and its
verbs, `payload` and `within` [D34].

A quote literal's content is read with the text around it, so a literal
that holds no pipeline is a syntax error, and `~()` is the empty quote,
which runs as the identity, `5 | apply ~()` answering `5`. A quote runs
through the tree it was read from, or through the parse of its printed
text, kept on the quote. `parse` flips text and a quote,
`"add 1" | parse` answering `~(add 1)` and `~(add 1 | mul 2) | parse`
answering `"add 1 | mul 2"`, and a trail prints its skipped steps
without the `|` of their head. `tag` puts a value under a tag through
the tag's constructor where the tag carries one, as it builds a quote
from a vector of steps, `[1 2 1] | tag ::set` answering `#[1 2]`. The
constructor of every record and
wrapper checks the fields the printer reads against the record's
schema, prints the step and reads the text back, so an assembly that
reads back as another step, or as none, is refused where it is made,
`::call{:name "add"}` answering `::call/PayloadNotSchemaError` and a
text that reads back otherwise `::call/ReadBackDiffersError`, and the
printer prints what text can produce and nothing more. `::fail` and a
declaration stand in a pipeline alone, so inside a container, a field
or a payload a step is an element step. `parse` and its inverse
round-trip every example of the catalog, which a test written in qlang
holds.
