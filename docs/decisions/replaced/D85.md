# D85 · The trail of an error is its path, a stop at every step that handed it on

Left at d1ebd70d, the tree this text was last true of: a path, a case or a
probe it names reads there, `git show d1ebd70d:<path>`, not in today's.

Domain. Language.
Decision. The `:trail` of an error is the path it took, a vector of
stops from the step where it arose to the step where it is read. A stop
is `{:step :subject :skipped}`: a step that raised the error or handed
it on, the subject that step had, and the steps of its pipeline the
error skipped after it. A step whose answer is a failure its subject
was not adds a stop: a step that fails, `raise`, and a step that
answers a failure from inside it, the call of a word, a group, a word
running a quote; a step the failure skips joins the `:skipped` of the
last stop. `/trail/0` is where the error arose and `/trail/-1` the stop
of the level that reads it, and `/trail/-1/skipped` replays there, so
every level has its own subject and its own skipped steps, and a failure
no step handles carries the path of the whole computation. The error
keeps the tag its site gave it, and the facts of its site come before
the trail. An error at rest that holds a `:trail`, raised, resumes that
path and adds no stop, so an error printed and read back, or built back
from its atoms [D42], is the error it was. `:faultStep` and
`:faultInput` leave the descriptor, the first stop saying what they
said.
Source. «мне кажется вычисленная цепочка ошибок должна как хлебные
крошки проводить по всей цепочке» and «может быть это сложнее выглядит -
но это честнее что ли перед пользователями и тогда в необрабатываемой
нигде ошибке будет содержаться фактически дамп всего вычисления»

(maintainer, 2026-09-26 07:22, session 86982eb5); on this one:
