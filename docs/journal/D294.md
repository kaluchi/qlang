# D294 · Open: the page a reader meets at a refusal and repairs a call by, and the axes that read it

Domain. Language.
Question. What does the page behind a refusal hold, how does a reader
reach it, a misspelled name among the roads, and how does a reader
assemble the description a question needs? A refusal is named by the
address of its site, and its page is prose with laws, the prose of the
catalog saying in places what the facts say:

```qlang
> "hello" | add 1 !| explain | page | content | contains "sumOf"
true
```

A reader who repairs a call wants of the page a procedure: what the
refusal means in one sentence, which fact names the culprit, the usual
cause, how to recover with `!|` as a runnable example, and the refusals
it is often confused with, the text refusals for one reason share written
once, which asks for a hierarchy of tags. A parse failure prints within
the excerpt of an alert [QSHST#answer], every token the parser could read
and the place it stopped:

```sh
$ node cli/src/bin.mjs '::a.b' < /dev/null 2>&1 | grep -c ':location ::span'
1
```

A tag nothing declares whose last name ends in `Error` reads the record
of the address before it, a refusal the runtime raises by rule having no
node of its own [QSSLF#axis], so a misspelled refusal reads the page of
its word with no refusal and no `:nearest`, while the same slip without
the suffix is refused with the name near it. `runLaws` and `doc` take
the anchor of a member and `laws` and `tutorial` refuse one
[QSSLF#laws], and `tutorial`, written in the language, refuses a name
nothing declares under the address of `doc`:

```qlang
> ::number/add/SubjectNotNumbrError | doc | eq (::number/add | doc)
true

> ::number/add/SubjectNotNumbrErr | doc !| type
::any/doc/UnknownNameError

> ::qlang | runLaws :errors | count
9

> ::qlang | laws :errors !| type
::any/laws/ArgsBeyondInputsError

> ::nothere | tutorial !| type
::any/doc/UnknownNameError
```

And the description a question needs is assembled from the definitions
under an address in more than one round trip, a value shared across
kinds of record carrying no tag of its own. The catalog says one fact
in several places: laws that nearly repeat each other, pages of kinds
that repeat the pages of their words, pages of words that repeat the
repair the pages of their refusals give, and one fact told in other
words on the pages of a vector, a set and a map; and the words of
`any.qlang` and of the three containers stand in an order that does not
follow what rests on what. The ninth outside audit,
`docs/journal/research/outside-audit/D327~1.md`, found two more: a tag
nothing declares asked of `is` answers false with no name near it, a
tag no value stands under being as good as a slip, and the prose of a
page keeps the indentation of its source, which `doc` and `explain`
print:

```qlang
> get [~(5 | is ::Numbr) ~(::vec/sortt | doc !| type)]
[false ::any/doc/UnknownNameError]

> ::vec/payload | doc | first | contains "\n            "
true
```

The words that combine the values of one subject, written in the
language, refuse a part their paths read under the word their recipe
calls, an address the reader never wrote, while a value given at the call
is refused under the input that takes it; and they take a quote apart:
`sumOf`, `subOf`, `mulOf` and `divOf` refuse one as a value of no kind of
their inputs, and `unionOf`, whose input names no kinds, runs it and
refuses a part of another kind by `union`:

```qlang
> {:b "x"} | get [~(sumOf 1 (get /b) !| get [~(type) /index]) ~(sumOf 1 /b !| get [~(type) /index])]
[[::any/sumOf/numbers/ElementKindNotTakenError 1] [::vec/sum/ElementNotNumberError 1]]

> {:x "1" :y 2} | get [~(subOf /x /y !| type) ~(mulOf /x /y !| type)]
[::number/sub/SubjectNotNumberError ::number/mul/multiplier/KindNotTakenError]

> {:a {:x 1}} | get [~(unionOf ~(get /a)) ~(sumOf ~(get /a/x) !| type) ~(unionOf 3 4 !| type)]
[{:x 1} ::any/sumOf/numbers/ElementKindNotTakenError ::call/KindAnswersNoVerbError]
```

The principles of the aim weigh it: a refusal gives the most useful
signal in the least room, its page the repair a reader needs, said once,
and a misspelled name answers the names near it and the page it was
likely meant to read, as every refusal does.
