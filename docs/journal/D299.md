# D299 · Open: what `coalesce` passes over, and where the subject stands

Domain. Language.
Question. `coalesce` answers the first alternative that is not null, and
the two rules its page states answer wrong where a reader errs. A group
computed at the call is a value, so a call of a group and a value puts
the subject first, and the subject, never null, answers; and code that
fails is taken as no value whatever the failure, the refusal of a name
the reader misspells among them:

```qlang
> {:t null} | coalesce (get /t) 30
{:t null}

> {} | coalesce ~(nosuchword) 30
30

> {:t null} | coalesce /t 30
30
```

Both are answers a reader cannot tell from a right one, which the first
attempt pays for, the measure of [the aim](AIM.md#mission).
Source. The outside audit of 32fc3a2e, its fourth problem,
`docs/journal/research/outside-audit/2026-10-15-32fc3a2e.md`, and the
fourth check of the chapters; the maintainer's word on the answer the
model had written into the chapter as a rule, «зачем ты занялся
легализацией qlang>  {:t null} | coalesce (get /t) 30» (maintainer,
2026-10-15 05:36, session 2e01c9cd).
Alternatives. One rule: an alternative stands in its place, the subject
first only where the call gives one value alone, `coalesce 30`, and code
passes to the next alternative only on null and on the refusal of a part
the subject lacks, `::path/KeyNotInMapError` and
`::path/IndexOutOfBoundsError`, any other failure answering the call; the
page and the section of `coalesce` change, and so do the cases that fall
back over another failure, `coalesce ~(1 | div 0) ~(2)` among them. The
subject first only through a form the reader writes, `coalesce ~(it) 30`,
every modifier an alternative in its place: the idiom
`query /t | coalesce 30` becomes `coalesce /t 30` across the catalog,
the cases and the first page. Or the rules as they stand.
