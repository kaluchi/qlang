# D313 · Open: the axes, a refusal raised by rule and the anchor of a member

Domain. Language.
Question. A tag nothing declares whose last name ends in `Error` reads
the record of the address before it, a refusal the runtime raises by
rule having no node of its own [QSSLF#axis], so a misspelled refusal
reads the page of its word with no refusal and no `:nearest`, while the
same slip without the suffix is refused with the name near it. `runLaws`
and `doc` take the anchor of a member and `laws` and `tutorial` refuse
one [QSSLF#laws], and `tutorial`, written in the language, refuses a
name nothing declares under the address of `doc`:

```qlang
> ::number/add/SubjectNotNumbrError | doc | eq (::number/add | doc)
true

> ::number/add/SubjectNotNumbrErr | doc !| type
::any/doc/UnknownNameError

> ::qlang | runLaws :errors | count
8

> ::qlang | laws :errors !| type
::any/laws/ArgsBeyondInputsError

> ::nothere | tutorial !| type
::any/doc/UnknownNameError
```
The principles of [D332] weigh it: a misspelled name answers the names
near it and the page it was likely meant to read, as every refusal does.
Source. The first check of the chapters, the report of self-description,
printing and hosts, `git show c91769c4:docs/journal/research/spec-check/2026-10-15-3d6ad533/`.
Alternatives. A refusal read through its word where the runtime raises
it by rule under that word alone, the last names the rules give, a
misspelled one refused with `:nearest`, at the cost of the axes knowing
the names the rules write. Every axis taking the anchor of a member, or
none but `doc`, at the cost of the inputs of the axes. A word written in
the language refusing under its own address, its recipe catching the
refusal of the word it calls, at the cost of a step in each such recipe.
Or the axes as they stand.
