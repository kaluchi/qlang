# D329 · Open: the kind of the elements of a container, one combinator for the contract of a word and the shape of a value

Domain. Language.
Question. How does a chain declare, and `shape` show, the kind of the
elements of a container, a set, a map whose keys are data or a
container of a kind of its own, and a key a record may lack? A chain
writes a tag as a kind, `#[…]` one of its kinds and, in `takes`, `wraps`
and `reads`, `[X]` a vector each of whose elements is of `X`; `returns`
and `carries` take no such form, so a word that answers a container says
its kind alone, `returns ::set` for `keys`, `kinds`, `manifest` and
`paths`, and a fact of a refusal is declared otherwise than it holds;
`shape` shows a set as a vector at the top and as `::set` alone below
it, and a key a record lacks as a key that holds null:

```qlang
> get [~(1 | add "x" !| get /expected) ~(::error/expected | binding | get /value)]
[#[::number] #[::tag]]

> ::fn | returns |~~ a ~~| [::number] !| type
::fn/returns/kinds/KindNotTakenError

> get [~({:a 1 :b 2} | keys | shape) ~({:tags #[:a]} | shape)]
[[::keyword] {:tags ::set}]

> [{:a 1} {}] | shape | eq ([{:a 1} {:a null}] | shape)
true
```

The tag of a container cannot stand over the shape of its elements: its
constructor reads the shape as data. `::set/of::number` is one tag the
constructor of `::set` never sees, and `::set::of::number` lays a value
of its own, `::of::number`, under `::set`, whose constructor takes a
vector alone:

```qlang
> :Box |~~ B ~~| (::kind | reads :w |~~ W ~~| /w ::number) | {:w ::number} | tag ::Box !| type
::Box/w/KindNotTakenError

> ::set[::keyword]
#[::keyword]

> ::set/of::number | type
::set/of

> ::set::of::number !| get [~(type) /expected]
[::set/PayloadKindNotTakenError #[::vec]]
```

The model's reading: the kind of a value stays its tag, the core the aim
holds, and a description of a container is a value of a kind of its
own, one for every container, the catalog's and a reader's alike, so
the contract of a word and the shape of a value write one fact one way.
