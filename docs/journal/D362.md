# D362 · Text is read into code by `parse` and code written as text by `toString`, a view leaves an elision, a regex goes by the word of its kind, a template answers itself, and a print reads back by `parse | open`

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The model, on the consilium that weighed parts 12, 15, 23, 30
and 33 of [D341], each part taken as its reconciler wrote it but where its
checker refuted it, there with the checker's repair, and where two parts
met over one word, as the pass over the records resolved them.

Decision.
- `parse` reads text alone, into the quote it writes, `"add 1" | parse`
  answering `~(add 1)`, a blank source the empty quote and a source that
  reads as no pipeline refused as `::quote/ParseError`. `toString` writes
  the text a value stands for: a number as JSON writes it, a keyword and a
  tag name by their names, a boolean and null by their literals, and a
  quote by its code, its steps in the one spelling the printer writes and
  without its brackets, `~(add 1 | mul 2) | toString` answering
  `"add 1 | mul 2"` and `"5 |  mul   2" | parse | toString` answering
  `"5 | mul 2"`, which `parse` reads back as the quote, so a quote turned
  into text and back is itself, as a number, a keyword and a tag name are.
  `print` writes the literal, `~(add 1) | print` answering `"~(add 1)"`,
  `json` the JSON of that literal, and `parseJson` reads JSON; the texts of
  a vector, a set and a map stay `print` and `json`, the `:nearest` of
  `toString` over them `[:json :print]`, and the text of a doc its
  `content`. `::quote/parse` leaves the language.
- A part a view left out gives way to an elision, a value of `::elision`,
  whose `:size` counts what it stands for and whose `:read` continues the
  query to it; marker names no value of the language, the grammar keeping
  the word for the signs of a comment and the terminal for the end of a
  paste. The tags of a shape over a part are its marks, `::absent` over a
  key some records lack and `::nullable` over a part null in some of the
  data, and a map whose keys are data folds into `::each` over its first
  key, a key that reads as an id, a date, a time, a uuid or an email among
  them; absent names the key a record lacks alone. At the move of the
  baseline the section `each` of [QSCOL], which tells the code `map` runs
  on each element and the images it answers, is named `image`, its file of
  cases with it.
- A regex is a value of the kind `::regex`, written `#"…"` or read from a
  string by `toRegex`. `match`, `capture` and `scan` take a regex alone,
  their input `:regex`, refusing a text as
  `::string/match/regex/KindNotTakenError`, while `contains`, `split` and
  `replace` take a regex or a text, told apart by its kind, `replace`
  naming what it seeks `:needle` as `contains` does. A string that writes
  no regex is refused by `toRegex` as `::string/toRegex/TextNotRegexError`.
  The module of the core that reads the source of a regex is named for it,
  the parser names a character and a flag of a regex among the
  continuations of a failure to parse, and the one order ranks a regex
  where it ranked a pattern. At the move of the baseline the chapter
  [QSTXT] is Text and regexes and its section `pattern` is named `regex`,
  its file of cases with it.
- A tuple is a vector of one length whose elements part index by index,
  which `shape` writes as a shape for each index, read index by index
  [QSPTH#verbs]; `get` over a template answers the template with each path
  read and each quote run, `{:price 4 :qty 3} | get [/price /qty]`
  answering `[4 3]`, and `query` reads the template softly, the laws and
  the cases that called that answer a tuple naming it the template.
- A print reads back as the value it writes by `parse | open`, `open`
  running the quote from null beneath its tags, `{:a 1} | print | parse |
  open` answering `{:a 1}` and `!{:a 1} | raise !| print | parse | open |
  raise !| keys` answering `#[:a :trace]`; `open` runs a link and a
  snippet alike, and `apply` runs code on a subject, `5 | apply ~(add 1)`.
  The texts, the laws and the cases that read a print back by
  `parse | apply it` read it by `parse | open`.
- The retired terms join the lexicon of retired terms
  [QSWRK#documents], each a pattern of its one sense: `::quote/parse`, the
  phrasings that call an elision a marker, the phrasings that call a regex
  a pattern, `TextNotPatternError` and the input `:pattern` of a verb of
  strings, the tuple a template answers, and `parse | apply it` for the
  read of a print; the generic words, marker, pattern and tuple, stay out
  of it.

```qlang
> ~(add 1 | mul 2) | get [~(toString) ~(print) ~(toString | parse | eq ~(add 1 | mul 2))]
["add 1 | mul 2" "~(add 1 | mul 2)" true]

> ~(add 1) | parse !| get [~(type) /addresses]
[::call/KindAnswersNoVerbError #[::string/parse]]

> get [~(#"a" | type) ~("(" | toRegex !| type) ~(::string/match | binding | get /value | inputs | map ~(get /name)) ~(::string/replace | binding | get /value | inputs | map ~(get /name))]
[::regex ::string/toRegex/TextNotRegexError [:regex] [:needle :with]]

> get [~({:a 1} | print | parse | open) ~(!{:a 1} | raise !| print | parse | open | raise !| keys)]
[{:a 1} #[:a :trace]]

> 100 | range | elide 10 | last | type
::elision
```

takes, its checker repairing each
(`docs/journal/research/design-check/D341~2.md`), and its pass over the
records resolved where they met
(`docs/journal/research/design-check/D353~1.md`): part 12 gave `parse` the
one direction of reading text and `toString` the text of a quote's code;
part 15 kept the elision of the kind's own image, its checker narrowing
marker to no value of the language, and the pass, measuring elision at
87% of its matches in the files naming `::elision` against 53% for marker,
left the grammar its word for the signs of a comment and took no
`:caret`, the place of a failure to parse being its `:location` alone;
part 23 named the regex for its kind, its checker adding the sections,
pages and case names that still said pattern; part 30 kept tuple for the
form of a shape; and part 33 kept one road to read a print back. Of the
read that turns text into code and back the maintainer wrote «для меня
это просто забавный эффект и не более...» (maintainer, 2026-10-12 18:36,
session 014e5a04), against the tree of that day, where `parse` read both
ways.
