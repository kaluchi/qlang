# D403 · A refusal of the grammar reads the form it refuses once a look ahead found it, a line doc's quote is tried as code within its line, and a dot before a key, a semicolon between queries and a chain on the line of its name are refused by name

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The model, carrying the open record [D295] in the
twenty-eighth batch of [D353], on the maintainer's words handing the open
records to the model and the questions of the consilium to the session,
which the source quotes; that a refusal looks ahead before it reads is the
rule of [QSSYN#refusal], which the tree missed.
Overrides [QSSYN#step], [QSSYN#combinator], [QSSYN#line], [QSSYN#doc],
[QSSYN#refusal], [QSLIT#number], [QSLIT#json], [QSLIT#closed]
Decision.
- A refusal the grammar writes reads the form it refuses only once a look
  ahead found it whole, so a form that is not there lends none of its
  continuations to another error: the declaration a tag name opens, a
  doc before a declaration, a tagged literal whose payload stands in
  parentheses, the quote of a path and a question mark after a segment of
  a path. A map is read once, its two spellings and an entry of two values
  refused where its entries end, and a comma after the pipeline of a group
  or of a quote is refused by the rule of the spacing alone, so a nest left
  open is read once at each depth. A question mark inside a quoted key is
  a letter of the key.
- A quote in a line doc is tried as code within its line alone, so a
  source that fails after a line doc fails where its reading stopped.
- A dot before a key, the path of jq, where a step or a modifier stands
  is refused naming the read by `get` and the path of its keys, `.users`
  naming `get /users`, and a dot alone naming `it`; a semicolon after a
  step is refused naming the template that asks several things in one
  query.
- A value after the commands that follow a declaration whose body is a tag
  name, the chain of a function or of a kind written on the line of its
  name, is refused naming the body of one step it read and the parentheses
  that hold a chain, and the line of its own a chain begins on where it is
  the body of a name with a path or with its page.
- The case of a bracket closed before the one inside it,
  `a-bracket-closed-before-the-one-inside-it-lists-the-continuations-where-it-stopped`,
  lists the continuations of the grammar alone, and the case of a number
  past the doubles, `a-number-literal-past-the-finite-doubles-is-refused-by-the-parser`,
  holds the refusal by its type, the order of the continuations being
  where the parser met them.

```qlang
> "(::Box ]" | parse !| get /expected
[:whitespace ":" "!|" "|" ")"]

> "[|~~| x ~(a\n 1 2 | f" | parse !| get /location
::span[17 2 6 18 2 7]

> "{:users 1} | .users" | parse !| get /message
"`.users` is no path: a part is read by `get` and a path, which opens with a slash, `get /users`"

> "::vec | doc; ::map | doc" | parse !| get /message | contains "one query asks several by a template"
true

> ":x ::Box | count | 5" | parse !| get /message | contains "is one step, `::Box`, and the commands after it are steps of the query"
true

> {:"?a" 1} | get /"?a"
1
```

Set aside.
- The continuations of a refused form kept beside those of the grammar, which
  name what a reader could not have meant.
- The refusals moved to a pass over the parser's tree, at the cost of the
  grammar written again and the place of each refusal found again.
- A body on the line of its name that runs on as a sentence where it opens
  with a tag name, which would read `:x ::Box | count` as another query.
