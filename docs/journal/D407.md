# D407 · Open: `elide` fills the budget a call gives it, a value it cut prints within the budget of every answer, and the refusal of an answer past that budget shows the most of it the budget holds

Domain. Language.
Question. How does `elide` fill the budget a call gives it, what does a
value it cut answer where the call gives more than the budget of every
answer, and how much of an answer past that budget does its refusal show?
`elide` keeps the first half of the parts of a vector, a set and a map,
and half of those until its print fits [QSPRN#elide], so a few parts large
beside the budget fill about half of it: of nine parts of a thousand
characters each, a budget of seven thousand keeps five and the elision of
the rest, where six fit whole.

```qlang
> 9 | range | map ~(get [~it ~(1000 | range | map ~("x") | join "")]) | elide 7000 | print | count | div 7000 | round 1
0.7

> 9 | range | map ~(get [~it ~(1000 | range | map ~("x") | join "")]) | elide 7000 | count
6
```

A call that gives more than the print of its subject answers the subject
as it is, and the host refuses an answer past the budget of every answer
whatever `elide` it went through; the refusal shows the head of the answer
within the excerpt of an alert, three hundred characters of a budget of
sixteen thousand [QSPRN#budget].

```qlang
> 100 | range | map ~(get [~it "abc"]) | :v it | elide 300000 | eq v
true
```

```sh
$ qlang '2000 | range | map ~(get [~it "abcdefghijklmnopqrstuvwxyz"])' < /dev/null | grep -v '^|~|' | wc -c
279
```

A value `elide` cut reads back from its print as itself, the value with
an `::elision` where a part was, and not as the data it was cut from, so
a print pasted into a cell or handed to a verb reads the hole; and an
elision of one string prints past a small budget, the hole costing more
than the room it was given:

```qlang
> ::verylognidentity{:s "a long line of text to leave out, which runs on long enough that its elision prints shorter than it"} | elide 20 | get [~(print | count) ~(get /s | type) ~(print | parse | open | get /s | type)]
[107 ::elision ::elision]
```

A value cut in the middle of a query is read as data by the steps after
it: over a table of ten thousand rows its count and a total read softly
answer numbers with no mark of the cut, a total read strictly is refused
as a missing key, and nothing names the cut:

```qlang
> 10000 | range | map ~(get {:id ~(it) :amount ~(mod 7)}) | :t it | get [~(t | map /amount | sum) ~(t | elide 4000 | count) ~(t | elide 4000 | map ~(query /amount | coalesce 0) | sum) ~(t | elide 4000 | map /amount | sum !| type)]
[29994 158 465 ::path/KeyNotInMapError]
```

The print of a value too large to print whole can be a view, a value of
a kind of its own, the name `::excerpt` standing for it until the
vocabulary weighs one [QSWRK#documents], whose payload is the quote of
its commands, built by a chain as a function is and written as its
literal as a link is, `::excerpt~(…)`: `of` names the holder of the
whole, `holds` the picture, the print of the whole as a quote with a mark
of its cut where each part was cut, along a seam of the parse of that
print, a run of parts or a part whole and never inside a word, and
`cut` each cut by its number and its label, its page. A quote builds
nothing, so a mark stands in the picture where a guard of a kind would
refuse it, its quotes written as they are; and the mark is a kind apart
from the view, `::elision`, whose page names it so today and whose
payload would be the number of its cut, lest one tag name both the view
the chain builds and the mark in its picture:

```qlang
> get [~(::fn | takes :by |~~ b ~~| /0 | print) ~(~(::trace[::frame[5 ::elision{}]]) | type) ~(::trace[::frame[5 ::elision{}]] !| type) ~(::elision#1 | type) ~(::link~(add 1) | payload | type)]
["::fn[~(takes :by |~~ b ~~| /0) null]" ::quote ::frame/PayloadNotSubjectAndStepsError ::elision ::quote]
```

The view prints as its literal and `| load`, which answers the whole
from the holder, so a print pasted into a cell answers the whole and the
reader goes on from it, and without `| load` answers the view, whose
`cuts` and `holds` read on; the chain binds no name, a cut being a
command held as data and its number the identity the mark names, as
Common Lisp prints shared structure by `#1=` and `#1#`, so nothing it
writes shadows the query after it. The details of a cut stand one step
deeper, with the holder, a map open to new keys: its span over the print
of the whole, a `::span` as the location of a parse failure is, the
elements it stands for and their shape, and the query that reads it; a
span, and not a path, being the address, since a path reads the
representation of a quote and of a doc where their prints show none,
`~(add 1 | mul 2) | get /1/modifiers/0`. The holder is an inhabitant of
the host [D424]: the command line, whose process ends with its answer,
keeps the print of the whole in both its modes under an identity of its
content, `::cli/Answer"7f3a9c"`, whose `load` answers the value, `text`
its print and `doc` the query and when it ran; a failure holds its
subjects in its trace; and a view printed where no holder is writes no
`of`, its `load` refused with the query that ran. The names of the parts
are the legend [D326]: the number of a cut and its label, the label one a
part bears wherever it is shown. The model's sketch, on the alert of a
failure over a hundred thousand rows, whose cut is the stretch
`::span[119 3 63 2188972 3 2188916]` of the print of the failure:

```text
|~| raised, and no !| caught it
::excerpt~(of ::cli/Answer"7f3a9c"
  | holds ~(::number/add/SubjectNotNumberError!{
        :actualType ::vec
        :trace ::trace[::frame[[{:id 0 :amount 0} {:id 1 :amount 1} ::elision#1] ~(add 1)]]})
  | cut 1 |~~ 99998 more maps the subject `add 1` met. ~~|)
| load

qlang> ::cli/Answer"7f3a9c" | cut 1
{:span ::span[119 3 63 2188972 3 2188916] :elements 99998
 :each {:id ::number :amount ::number} :read ~(…)}
```

What waits: at which stage the view is made, after the answer or the
failure is final, as the print of the host, and whether the view is the
refusal of a print past the budget or a part of it, a fact of
`::qlang/AnswerBeyondBudgetError`; the alert, whose subjects its own rule
cuts to excerpts within the budget [QSERR#alert], drawn on the same view;
the REPL, which prints an answer whole; and the name of the view.
