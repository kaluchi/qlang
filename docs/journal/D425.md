# D425 · Open: a value nested past the stack of its host, and the one depth the language would hold

Domain. Language.
Question. What does the language answer for a value nested deeper than the
stack of its host lets a walk of the core go? A query builds a value of any
depth and a JSON text a host reads gives one whole, while the printer,
`shape`, the codec of JSON, the one order and the reader walk a value by the
stack of the host, `collectPrintsOfKinds` in `core/src/runtime/print.mjs`
among the walks of the printer, so each fails past a depth of its own as a
failure of the host, and the command line, whose alert prints the subject of
that failure, ends with the message of the host and no answer; the door of a
host walks by a stack of its own, `foreignPartOf` in `core/src/dispatch.mjs`.
The text of a value reads back as it by `parse | open` [QSPRN#print], and a
cell keeps an answer past the budget as that text [D407], so the store of a
cell meets the hole first; a doc whose prose the block form cannot hold is
another hole of the read back, which [D369] holds.

```qlang
> get [~(100 | range | reduce [] ~(get [/0]) | :v it | print | parse | open | eq v) ~(20000 | range | reduce [] ~(get [/0]) | print !| tag)]
[true ::call/HostFailureError]

> 20000 | range | reduce [] ~(get [/0]) | :v it | get [~(print !| tag) ~(shape !| tag) ~(json !| tag) ~(eq v !| tag)]
[::call/HostFailureError ::call/HostFailureError ::call/HostFailureError ::call/HostFailureError]

> :l (5000 | range | map ~("[") | join "") | :r (5000 | range | map ~("]") | join "") | l | append r | parseJson | get [~(tag) ~(print !| tag)]
[::vec ::call/HostFailureError]
```

```sh
$ (qlang '20000 | range | reduce [] ~(get [/0]) | print | count' < /dev/null 2>&1; echo "exit $?") | grep -e '^qlang:' -e '^exit'
qlang: Maximum call stack size exceeded
exit 1
```

The depth each walk gives way at is the stack of the host's: on the
maintainer's machine, on 25 October 2026, through the command line, `print`
printed a vector nested nine hundred deep and failed at a thousand, `shape`
held at thirteen hundred and failed at sixteen hundred, `json` held at
sixteen hundred and failed at nineteen hundred, and `eq` held at two
thousand and failed at five thousand. The reader reads a literal two
thousand deep and refuses one four thousand deep as a parse failure whose
message is the host's, a text of the language refused as no text of it:

```qlang
> :l (2000 | range | map ~("[") | join "") | :r (2000 | range | map ~("]") | join "") | l | append r | parse | open | count
1

> :l (4000 | range | map ~("[") | join "") | :r (4000 | range | map ~("]") | join "") | l | append r | parse !| get [~(tag) ~(message)]
[::quote/ParseError "Maximum call stack size exceeded"]
```

Alternatives. The roads, each with its cost.
- One depth the language holds, the idea the maintainer keeps, on his word
  that no model reads a nesting of some thousands of levels: a constant of
  its own as the depth of the evaluation is,
  `::call/EvaluationDepthExceededError`, below the depth every walk of the
  core holds on every host, five hundred and twelve levels on the model's
  reading of the depths above; the reader, the codec of JSON and the door
  of a host refuse a value nested past it, each by a refusal that names the
  depth and the limit, `print`, `shape`, `json` and the one order refuse so
  a value a query built past it, and the layers of an excerpt end at it, a
  part below them showing as its least excerpt, so no alert meets the stack
  of the host. At the cost of a count of the depth in the reader, the
  printer, the codec, `shape`, the order and the walk of the doors, of a
  text of JSON nested past the limit refused, and of a value built past it
  in a query that keeps no print and no cell. Or a depth the language
  bounds where a value is built, a check on every container built.
- The walks of the core and the reader of literals walking by a stack of
  their own, so a value prints and reads back at any depth the evaluator
  builds, or a second reader of the literals of data; set aside by the
  maintainer's word: a depth past what a model reads asks no print, and
  the grammar is mended in itself [D407].
- The walks as they stand, each failing past its depth as a failure of the
  host, a store keeping the text the core prints and a cell meeting the
  hole where a path passes its card and reads the answer back.
