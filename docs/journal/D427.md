# D427 · A value split and joined by one name for each side, the parts of a value named for one template by `with`, and the constructor of a kind no name of its own

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The maintainer, who took the constructor of a kind out of the
names, asked for a bijection of `tag` and `payload` and for a verb that
names the parts of a value for the template built from them, kept its two
forms, a vector of paths and a map, whose places are read against the
subject apart from each other, and set aside the join of a pair and the
form that names every key of a map; the forms are the model's: `tag`
reading where it is given no name, `type` the name a reader brings for that
read, the payload of a value of a kind of the core the value its canonical
literal writes after its tag, the name `with` from its ancestors and its
rules, a part named by the last key of its path, a map inside the vector
that renames, and the key a host hands a constructor under.
Overrides [QSBLD#kind], [QSBLD#label], [QSBLD#chain], [QSHST#source], [QSDSP#residence], [QSLIT#kind], [QSLIT#tag], [QSNAM#address], [QSPTH#template]
Decision.
1. The constructor of a kind lays no name of its own: the node
   `<label>/construct` and the page the runtime writes for it leave, the
   constructor standing as the second part of the literal of its kind,
   `::number | binding | get /value | get /1`, and running where its tag
   is laid, as a literal or by `tag`; a host hands it under the address of
   its label, `tag` where the core registers `'tag/construct'`, and the
   root takes it for the binding of the kind; `construct` is a name like
   any other under a label. The step of a tagged literal, `::construct`,
   stays, the word naming it alone.
2. `tag` given no name answers the tag of its subject, the outermost,
   `1 | tag` answering `::number` and `::A::B{} | tag` answering `::A`, and
   given a name lays it, as it does; the join of a pair, `[::Box 5] | tag`,
   leaves. `type` reads as `tag` reads, the name a reader brings, as `size`
   and `length` stand beside `count`. `payload` answers every value: the
   value beneath its outermost tag, and for a value of a kind of the core
   the value its canonical literal writes after the tag, `1 | payload`
   answering `1`, `{:a 1} | payload` `{:a 1}` and `#[2 1] | payload`
   `[1 2]`; so `:t (tag) | payload | tag t` answers the value it is given,
   every value.
3. `with`, a verb of every value, names parts of its subject and answers
   its body, a template as `get` takes it, a quote among them, each quote
   of it run against the subject with the names declared in it under the
   rule of a fork, so no name leaves the call and the quotes see nothing of
   each other but the names. The names come in two forms, as the select
   list of SQL names its columns: a vector of paths, each part named by the
   last key of its path,
   `{:id 7 :user {:name "Ann"}} | with [/id /user/name] ~(name | append (id | toString))`
   answering `"Ann7"`; and a map, each key the name of what its place
   answers, `[::Box 5] | with {:t ~first :p ~last} [~(t) ~(p) ~(p | tag t)]`
   answering `[::Box 5 ::Box#5]`, a map inside the vector renaming the
   parts it maps, `with [/id {:un /user/name :gn /group/name}] …`. A place
   is a path or a quote, each read against the subject as `get` reads the
   parts of a template, apart from each other: a path strictly and a quote
   as it is written, `{:t ~(query /type "none")}` reading softly, a shape or
   a literal written in a quote, `{:pair ~(get [/1 /0])}`; so a map of the
   data names no parts, `{:a 1 :b 2} | with it …` refused, and the keys of a
   record are named by their paths. No place sees the name of another, so
   the maps of a vector are one map in any order,
   `with [{:t ~first} {:p ~last}] …` naming what
   `with [{:p ~last} {:t ~first}] …` names, and a quote alone in the vector
   names no part. A name written twice, a last key among them,
   `with [/a/name /b/name] …`, and a last key that reads as no name of one
   segment by the letters of a name [QSNAM#letters], an index, a quoted key
   or a word of the literals, are refused under `with`, its page naming the
   map that renames them, and a name with a path is an address
   [QSNAM#address], so `user/name` names no part. A name that meets a verb
   serving the subject is refused aloud, as every declaration that meets
   one is [QSNAM#reach],
   `{:type "user" :id 7} | with [/id /type] ~(id | toString | append type)`
   refused as `::call/ValueMeetsVerbError`, and the map renames the part,
   `with [/id {:t /type}] ~(id | toString | append t)` answering `"7user"`;
   a key the data holds and no name names is no name, so
   `{:items [1 2] :count 9} | with [/items] ~(items | count)` answers `2`.

What stands today, which the decision moves:

```qlang
> get [~(1 | tag !| type) ~(1 | payload !| type) ~(::number/construct | binding | get /value | type)]
[::any/tag/SubjectNotPairError ::tagged/payload/SubjectNotTaggedError ::builtin]

> get [~([::Box 5] | tag) ~([::Box 5] | :t (get /0) | get /1 | tag t) ~({:a 1 :b 2} | apply ~(:a (get /a) | :b (get /b) | a | mul b))]
[::Box#5 ::Box#5 2]
```

A prototype of `with` on a copy of the core outside the tree, its names
declared in front of its body, answered the examples above and left no name
in the scope of the session; a first draft of it read its places one after
another, so a place saw the name of one before it and two orders of the
maps answered apart, and parsed its body again, so the names of the step
that wrote the body were withheld from it, neither of which the decision
takes: the places are read apart, and the body is code of the step that
wrote it [QSQUO#held]. A name is told by the letters of a name the core
reads, `core/src/name-chars.mjs`, one segment and no word of the literals,
as the chain of a function tells the name of an input.
Set aside.
- The form of one input that names every key of a map that reads as a
  name, `with ~(…)`, the `select *` of SQL: names the data chooses, which
  meet a verb the code calls on one record of a vector and on no other,
  `[{:items [1]} {:items [2 3] :count 9}] | map ~(with ~(items | count))`
  refused on the second record by a prototype and answering `[1 9]` on a
  copy that let the key take the name of the verb; set aside by the
  maintainer's word.
- Names declared in the pipeline in front of a template, which run today:
  a head of declarations, whose names stay in the scope of the cell after
  it, `[::Box 5] | :t (get /0) | :p (get /1) | get [~(p | tag t)]` leaving
  `env | keys` answering `#[:p :t]`; set aside by the maintainer's word.
- A vector of keywords, `with [:a :b :c :d] …`: the keys of one level under
  their own names, which a vector of paths names and reaches deeper; set
  aside by the maintainer's word.
- The code as a second input of `get`, `get {:t /type} ~(…)`: `query` holds
  its default in that place, a quote among them,
  `{:a 1} | query /b ~(add 1)` answering `~(add 1)`, so the soft read would
  not follow it; the maintainer kept `with`.
- A pattern in a declaration, `:[t p] it` and `:{id title} it`: signs no
  reader takes in at once, set aside by the maintainer's word.
- A step `let` that declares the names of a template for the steps after
  it: a form of the grammar and names that outlive the one use a record
  is put to.
- `apply` declaring the keys of a map it is given: a verb whose rule stays
  as it is, set aside by the maintainer's word for a verb of its own.
- The keys of a map read by the words of their names wherever a step
  meets them [D326]: a key and a word in one space of names.
- The join of a pair kept beside the name `tag` reads: two answers of one
  call told apart by the kind of the subject, the join written by a name
  declared, `:t (get /0) | get /1 | tag t`, which no reader of the catalog
  but the laws of `tag` itself needs.
