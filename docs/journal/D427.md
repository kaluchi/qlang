# D427 · A value split and joined by one name for each side, the names of a record mounted for one quote by `with`, and the constructor of a kind no name of its own

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The maintainer, who took the constructor of a kind out of the
names, asked for a bijection of `tag` and `payload` and for a verb that
mounts the names of a record for one quote, and set the join of a pair
aside; the forms are the model's: `tag` reading where it is given no name,
`type` the name a reader brings for that read, the payload of a value of a
kind of the core the value its canonical literal writes after its tag, the
name `with` from its ancestors and its rules, and the key a host hands a
constructor under.
Overrides [QSBLD#kind], [QSBLD#label], [QSBLD#chain], [QSHST#source], [QSDSP#residence], [QSLIT#kind], [QSLIT#tag], [QSNAM#address], [QSCOL#within], [QSQUO#apply]
Decision.
1. The constructor of a kind lays no name of its own: the node
   `<label>/construct` and the page the runtime writes for it leave, the
   constructor standing as the second part of the literal of its kind,
   `::number | binding | get /value | get /1`, and running where its tag
   is laid, as a literal or by `tag`; a host hands it under the address of
   its label, `tag` where the core registers `'tag/construct'`, and the
   root takes it for the binding of the kind; `construct` is a name like
   any other under a label. The step of a tagged literal, `::construct`,
   stays, the word naming it alone.
2. `tag` given no name answers the tag of its subject, the outermost,
   `1 | tag` answering `::number` and `::A::B{} | tag` answering `::A`, and
   given a name lays it, as it does; the join of a pair, `[::Box 5] | tag`,
   leaves. `type` reads as `tag` reads, the name a reader brings, as `size`
   and `length` stand beside `count`. `payload` answers every value: the
   value beneath its outermost tag, and for a value of a kind of the core
   the value its canonical literal writes after the tag, `1 | payload`
   answering `1`, `{:a 1} | payload` `{:a 1}` and `#[2 1] | payload`
   `[1 2]`; so `:t (tag) | payload | tag t` answers the value it is given,
   every value.
3. `with`, a verb of a map, runs the code its call gives on the map with
   each key that reads as a bare name declared before the code, as a
   reader declares names by hand, under the rule of a fork, so no name
   leaves the code: `{:a 1 :b 2} | with ~(a | mul b)` answers `2`, and
   `[::Box 5] | get {:t /0 :p /1} | with ~(p | tag t)` answers `::Box#5`. A
   name that meets a verb serving the subject is refused aloud, as every
   declaration that meets one is, `{:count 3} | with ~(count)` refused as
   `::call/ValueMeetsVerbError`, so the key `type` a JSON text often
   holds, which a verb of every value names, is read by its path, `{:type
   "user" :id 7} | with ~(get /type | append (id | toString))` answering
   `"user7"`, and the verb read by its address; a key that reads as no bare
   name is read by its path inside the code; and a value under the tag of a kind mounts
   the parts its kind names, never the keys its tag guards.

What stands today, which the decision moves:

```qlang
> get [~(1 | tag !| type) ~(1 | payload !| type) ~({:a 1} | with ~(a) !| type) ~(::number/construct | binding | get /value | type)]
[::any/tag/SubjectNotPairError ::tagged/payload/SubjectNotTaggedError ::call/UnknownNameError ::builtin]

> get [~([::Box 5] | tag) ~([::Box 5] | :t (get /0) | get /1 | tag t) ~({:a 1 :b 2} | apply ~(:a (get /a) | :b (get /b) | a | mul b))]
[::Box#5 ::Box#5 2]
```

A prototype of `with` on a copy of the core outside the tree, the keys
declared in front of the code, answered the two examples above, `3` for
`{:a 1 :b 2} | with ~(get /a | add b)`, the refusal of `count`, no name
after the code and a key that is no name read by its path, and refused a
subject that is no map as `::map/with/SubjectNotMapError`, while `within`
refuses a value under no tag; on a map under the tag of no kind it read
the keys beneath the tag, which the decision moves to the parts a kind
names.
Set aside.
- A pattern in a declaration, `:[t p] it` and `:{id title} it`: signs no
  reader takes in at once, set aside by the maintainer's word.
- A step `let` that declares the names of a template for the steps after
  it: a form of the grammar and names that outlive the one use a record
  is put to.
- `apply` declaring the keys of a map it is given: a verb whose rule stays
  as it is, set aside by the maintainer's word for a verb of its own.
- The keys of a map read by the words of their names wherever a step
  meets them [D326]: a key and a word in one space of names.
- The join of a pair kept beside the name `tag` reads: two answers of one
  call told apart by the kind of the subject, the join written by a name
  declared, `:t (get /0) | get /1 | tag t`, which no reader of the catalog
  but the laws of `tag` itself needs.

The name `with` is that of R,
`with(data, expr)`, of Pascal's `with r do` and of Kotlin's `with(obj) { }`,
an expression run in an environment made of a record.
