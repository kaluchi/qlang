# D466 · Open: the verdict of a verb, its slots poured under the names the catalog gives, one level under its holder

Domain. Language.
Question. How does a refusal tell its facts so that each key reads as an
address of the catalog and names what a declaration already names? A key
of a failure a host prints reads as the address of its tag and the key,
`::number/add/SubjectNotNumberError/trace` a node the catalog holds, and a
refusal is laid two ways today: one level under its holder,
`::number/add/SubjectNotNumberError` and `ArgsBeyondInputsError` beside the
inputs of `add`, or under a slot of it, `::number/add/addend/KindNotTakenError`,
the input refusing as if it judged, while its facts hang one level under the
refusal with the pages of a family of twelve, `expected` “The kinds taken.”.
`actualValue` names eight things by the stage that refused: the args of a
call past its inputs and of an input not given, an input, an element, an
answer, a payload, the value of a field, the failure a selector met and the
value under its tag an invariant of a kind refused; a refusal of the subject
holds none, the subject lying in the first frame of its trace, and a refusal
a kind lays after the verb ran, `::number/NotFiniteError`, holds its trace
alone. A key past a wired name, `…/trace/failed`, reads as no address.

reading of the rest standing beside them for the session after the
compaction to verify against the tree:
- the verdict is the verb's, never an input's or a modifier's, the inputs
  long assembled when it judges, and it stands one level under the verb,
  `::number/add/<Verdict>Error`; a kind's door judges a value it lays the
  same way, one level under the kind;
- its facts are one flat list of keys, each the name of a slot the verb
  declares and a side of it, `subject/expected`, `subject/actualTag`,
  `addend/actualValue`, the names of the catalog reused and none of the
  verdict's own, so the key and the tag read as one address,
  `::number/add/SubjectNotNumberError/subject/expected`;
- the verdict pours every slot the verb assembled, so the reader sees what
  went where: `subject`, the inputs in the order the chain declares them,

  computed; a kind pours its fields, `payload` where it wires none, and its
  invariants are the door's verdicts, those of a verb its verdicts on a

Alternatives. The points the road leaves open, each with its cost.
- The live reading of a side of a slot. A name with a path in a step is an
  address, `!| addend/expected` calling `::addend/expected`, and a kind
  wires names of one segment alone. The refusal wires each slot as one name
  holding its sides, `!| addend | get /actualValue`, the print laying them
  flat, `:addend/actualValue`: at the cost of a live form and a printed form
  a step apart. Or the facts flat in the map of the error at rest, read by
  `payload | get /:addend/actualValue`: at the cost of a path beneath a
  closed tag, which a wired tag refuses [QSPTH#get]. Or a name with a path
  wired by a kind and read bare: at the cost of a new reading of a step.
- The views of a value past the room as sides of its slot. `…/addend/count`
  reads through the kind of the value refused, which no address names: the
  views of `::any` alone, `tag` and `shape`, at the cost of the count and
  the two ends; or the sides the kind of the value names at the print, at
  the cost of a key that reads as no address.
- The name of a verdict on a slot of one kind: `<Slot>KindNotTakenError`,
  one rule for any count of kinds, or `<Slot>Not<Kind>Error`, read as
  `SubjectNotNumberError` reads, at the cost of two forms by the count of
  kinds.
- A slot that passed beside the one refused: each with its `actualValue`
  only, as the road takes it, or each with its three sides, at the cost of
  sides that tell nothing of a slot that held.
