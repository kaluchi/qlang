# D478 · The print of a value is the language's literal: a kind declares no print of its own, and a legend lays its glosses over the literal where an answer is drawn

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The maintainer: a kind declares no print of its own; what a
host prints of an answer, the stand-in it puts in its place and its
painting, the host's concern; and the legend laid over the data. The model:
the refusal of a `print` declared under a label, the glosses of a legend
laid over the literal by the layer that draws an answer, the printer of the
literal knowing no legend, and the cases, laws and tests that held on a
kind's own print.
Overrides [QSPRN#print], [QSPRN#legend], [QSHST#effect], [QSDSP#tagname]
Decision.
- The literal of a value is the language's, written by the printer of the
  core and read back as the value, whatever its kind. A `print` declared
  under a label, a kind of a reader, of a host or of the root, is refused,
  `::bind/PrintDeclaredError`, `:name` naming it; a view of a value for
  reading is a verb of its own name, `legend` among them.
- What a host prints of an answer is drawn over that literal: the line
  `|~| raised, and no !| caught it` over a failure; past the budget of
  every answer the stand-in, the alert that keeps the answer in a cell and
  the excerpt of a fact past its room, which read back as themselves, the
  cell holding what they stand for [QSPRN#budget], [QSPRN#excerpt]; and the
  glosses of a legend laid over the parts of the value it tells as
  comments, which read back as whitespace; `print` answers that drawing
  whole, and its painting is the medium's. The layer that draws it,
  `printAnswer` and `boundAnswer`, knows the legend; the printer of the
  literal knows none, a part drawn ahead of it standing on lines of its
  own.
- The rule that passes a tag name to the verb of its name on `::any`
  [QSDSP#tagname] reads by a verb of the reader's own, `:any/label` beside
  `:Box/label`, where it read by `print`.

```qlang
> :Box |~~ B ~~| | :Box/print ~("box") | [::Box#1 ::Box] | print
::bind/PrintDeclaredError!{ :name :Box/print … }

> :number/print ~("n") | get [~([::Box#5 7] | print) ~(7 | print)]
::bind/PrintDeclaredError!{ :name :number/print … }
```

```sh
$ qplay ':Box |~~ B ~~| | :Box/print ~("box") | [::Box#1] | tap :x | count' < /dev/null 2>&1
|~| raised, and no !| caught it
::bind/PrintDeclaredError!{
  :name :Box/print
  :trace ::trace[::frame[::qlang ~(:Box/print ~("box") | [::Box#1] | tap :x | count)]]
}
|~| The print of a value is the language's literal, whatever its kind,
|~| the text that reads back as the value, which a host shows for an
|~| answer and `print` answers, so a `print` declared under a label is
|~| refused, `:name` naming it. A view of a value for reading is a verb
|~| of its own name under the label, `:Box/label ~(get /name)`, which a
|~| reader asks for, `legend` the view of the language that glosses each
|~| part. ::link~(::any/print) ::link~(::any/legend) ::link~(::bind)
```

Set aside.
- A print of its own kept for the kinds of the language, the legend among
  them, and refused to a reader and to a host by the source of its
  declaration: two rules for one door.
- The legend a text, the literal laid out with its glosses answered as a
  string: a string in a template stands in quotes with its line breaks
  escaped, and the playground prints the text of a string unpainted.
