# D488 · The parser reads a place once: a word under a tag reads its tag once and what touches it after it, a name of segments joined by `/` is read once, a letter of a name reads its code point in its own rule, a comment is tried where its `|` stands, and a bare keyword is read in one pass

Domain. Implementation.
Decided by. The model, on the maintainer's word that asks for the speed of
the start in this stint, with the measures of the session the source names
[D417].
Decision.
- A word under a tag reads its tag once and then what touches it: the error
  literal under the tag, `::Tag!{…}`, a payload after `#`, the refusals of a
  payload or of code in parentheses, a payload by its own sign, or the tag
  alone; the memory of a failure [D404] keeps the offset after the tag, where
  what touches it failed. The word under a tag stands before the error
  literal among the words and among the payloads of a tag, where the error
  literal read its tag first, so a refusal names its continuations in the
  order it named them.
- A name of segments joined by `/` is read once, after `:`, after `::` and
  as the name of a call, its segments in one loop, where an alternative that
  read the first segment and failed for want of a `/` gave way to one that
  read it again.
- A letter of a name reads its code point in its own rule, and a comment is
  tried where its `|` stands.
- A bare keyword is read by one expression of the letters of a name, where
  its segments and the letters of each were read one by one.
- Over the catalog the parser enters its rules 2.27 million times where it
  entered them 4.22 million times, a rule at an offset 1.23 times where it
  entered it 1.95 times; the start of the playground answering `1`
  takes 981 ms at the least of twelve runs where it took 1079 ms, beside it
  on one disk.

```qlang
> get [~(::Box[1 2]) ~(::Width#5) ~([::a/b :c/d/e ::vec/count] | map ~tag) ~(:x/y) ~([1 2] | vec/count) ~(::A::B!{}) ~(::error!{:x 1})]
[::Box[1 2] ::Width#5 [::tag ::keyword ::tag] :x/y 2 ::A::B!{} !{:x 1}]

> "::Width(5)" | parse !| get [~message ~(location | start | offset)]
["a number, a boolean, null or a path touches its tag by `#`: write `::Width#5`" 0]
```
Source. The maintainer saw the start grow, «и я заметил по длительности
тестов просадка произошла … раньше на 0.7-0.8 секунд весь каталог
парсило ..» (maintainer, 2026-10-29 06:29 UTC, session ac8a358c), weighed
the speed of the parser, «780K символов каталога - это меньше чем
(~1.9 МБ/с)» (06:44), and asked for it here, «в этой смене ускорение
сделай, потом в конце будем бенчарки крутить» (06:56). The measures of
the session: the start grew with the catalog, 365 ms over 122K characters on
2026-09-12, 573 ms over 524K on 2026-10-12 and 1073 ms over 781K at the
baseline, the steepest step the branch that wrote every word as a chain;
the parse of the catalog was half of the start, 535 ms cold, and the rules
the parser entered again at an offset were mostly the readings of a tag, up
to fourteen for a tag alone, and of a name before a `/` it lacked. The
previous grammar and this one read 43640 texts alike, the catalog, the
queries of the cases and the strings they hold, the probes of the chapters
and of the records and mutations of each, 21240 read part by part to the
same spans and values and 22400 refused with the same message, place,
continuations and character found; the bare keyword read in one pass and
segment by segment answered 160651 generated names alike.
Set aside.
- The cache of peggy, which keeps every rule at every offset: the parse
  three times slower.
- The flags of V8 and the compile cache of Node: the start no faster.
- A page read where it is first asked: the pages are half of the catalog and
  a fifth of its parse, and finding where a page ends without reading it is a
  second reading of a doc.
- A table of the letters among the first 128 code points: ten milliseconds,
  and a text of more than one character answered otherwise by the exported
  readers of a letter.
Requirements. The suites of every workspace and the probes as they stood;
the tests of [D404], which read every shape in a time linear in its size.
