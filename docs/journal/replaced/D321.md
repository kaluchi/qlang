# D321 · A word under the name another language gives it, where the habit expects its answer

Left at 131c4622, the tree this text was last true of: a path, a case or a
probe it names reads there, `git show 131c4622:<path>`, not in today's.

Domain. Language.
Decided by. The maintainer, that a name a reader's habit reaches for
enters beside the word whose answer the habit expects, where the
expectation is the same; which names enter, each a function whose recipe
calls the word, and `concat` a word of its own, are the model's.
Overrides [QSWRK#catalog], [QSCOL#sequence], [QSCOL#map],
[QSCOL#algebra], [QSTXT#string]
Decision. A name a reader trained on other languages reaches for enters
the catalog beside the word that answers what the habit expects, as
`distinct` stands beside `toSet`: a function under the same label whose
recipe calls the word, its page naming the word, so the fact keeps one
spelling and the name is another road to it. Where the habit expects
another answer or another call, the name is a word of its own or none,
since a name that answers otherwise than the habit expects fails in
silence. `flatten` answers as `flat` over a vector and a set, one level
spliced in; `merge` as `union` over maps, the other map winning a key
both hold; and `size` and `length` as `count` over a vector, a set, a map
and a string:

```qlang
> get [~([[1 2] [3 [4]]] | flatten) ~({:a 1 :b 2} | merge {:b 3 :c 4} | get /b) ~("abc" | length) ~(#[1 2] | size)]
[[1 2 3 [4]] 3 3 2]
```

`concat`, which joins the vectors the call gives after the subject, is a
word of its own, since no word joined two vectors and the habit calls it
with the vectors to join:

```qlang
> get [~([1] | concat [2 3] [4]) ~([[1] [2 3]] | reduce [] ~(concat))]
[[1 2 3 4] [1 2 3]]
```

`find`, the first element a condition holds of, and `slice`, a range of
places, wait for a task.

Set aside. The page of a word naming the words of other languages, a
role of its doc that `:nearest` reads, under which a round trip stays
each time; `concat` an alias of `flat`, which would answer the habit's
call, `[1] | concat [2]`, with a refusal of its args; `flatten` deep, as
Ruby and Clojure read it, against `flat` and the reading of JavaScript and
lodash; and the refusals as they stood, a round trip each.
Requirements. The cases of D321: `flatten` answers as `flat` over a
vector and a set; `merge` as `union` over maps, the other map winning a
key both hold; `size` and `length` as `count` over a vector, a set, a map
and a string; and `concat` joins the vectors after the subject, folds the
vectors a vector holds by `reduce [] ~(concat)`, answers under the tag of
its subject and refuses an element that is no vector.
