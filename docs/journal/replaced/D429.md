# D429 · The session, the stint and its closeout

Domain. Process.
Decided by. The maintainer: a closeout at his word or in a session that
works on its own while he is away, every text of the journal a stint
writes landing on its branch, and the units of the work parted by words of
their own. The model's: the words, a session, a stint, a closeout and the
reconciliation, and the ceiling of the records since the baseline leaving

Overrides [QSWRK#journal], [QSWRK#stint], [QSWRK#closeout],
[QSWRK#reconciliation]
Decision.
- The work moves in three units, each under a word of its own. A session
  is one conversation of a model, the unit of its context. A stint is one
  branch, from the clean desk the last closeout left to the next, as many
  sessions and days as its work takes, the branch named after the scars of
  its stint. A closeout ends a stint: its records dissolve, its chapters are
  written, its structure is cut anew where its work asks, and its branch
  lands on master by a merge.
- A closeout comes at the maintainer's word, or in a session that works on
  its own while he is away. No count of records and no end of a session
  asks for one: a session ends with no rite, each decision written as a
  record when it is taken, and the ceiling of twenty records since the
  baseline leaves.
- Every text of the journal a stint writes lands on its branch, an open
  record, a line of fate, the aim, a report of the research and its reading
  among them, and master takes them with the merge. A subagent that works
  in a worktree commits on a branch of its own, which its stint takes back.
  Nothing in a stint switches the branch of the checkout, merges or writes
  to master but its closeout.
- The checks that hold what a closeout leaves, from one closeout to the
  next, are the reconciliation of the tree with its baseline,
  `git show 95169244:scripts/reconciliation.mjs`, which `check:conventions` fails on and the
  desk shows.
- A shift, the move of the baseline and the walk of the baseline are
  retired for a stint, a closeout and the reconciliation, each in the
  lexicon of retired terms.
