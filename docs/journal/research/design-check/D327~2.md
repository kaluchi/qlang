## 1. The view a budget makes

Agreement 2/3, size M.

**Road.** A view that cuts every kind and keeps the head alone. Every container keeps its first parts and a marker of the rest, a map's under the key `:"…"`. A value under a tag is cut as its payload. A text, and a part a kind prints, give way to a marker with their head wherever they stand.


**Decision.** `elide` cuts each part by its kind, at the top as beneath it. A value under a tag is cut as its payload, with the tag kept over the cut.
- A vector, a set and a map keep their first parts and a marker of the rest. The marker's `:read` drops the parts kept, `~(drop 12)`. They halve until the view fits. A vector and a map keep their order, and a set stands in the one order.
- A map's marker of the rest stands under the key `:"…"`. The data may hold that key among the entries the view would keep. Then the view keeps the entries before it, so that entry is the first of the rest, which `drop` reads, and the key stands in the view for the marker alone.
- The top keeps its first part at the least. Beneath the top, a container gives way whole at the last.
- A string, a doc, a quote, and a part a kind prints its own way, give way whole to a marker, at the top as beneath it. The marker keeps under `:head` the first characters of their text, or of the print the kind gives. The marker of the top is read by the empty quote, `~()`.

Every part a view keeps stands at the path that reads it in the value. A view is built beneath the constructors. So where a view put a marker in a part a kind checks, its print reads back refused by that kind, as a marker in a field a kind checks reads back refused today.


**Why.** The deciding principle is that looking into an answer is safe: where a literal does not print within its bound, the language compresses it and says what it left out and how to read it. Today two kinds of value break that:
- A map and a text at the top pass every budget: `1000 | range | map ~(get [~(it) ~(it)]) | toMap | get [~(print | count) ~(elide 2000 | print | count)]` answers `[12783 12783]`.
- A container under a tag passes any budget: `300 | range | tag ::Box | elide 100 | print | count` answers 1096.
The refusal of part 5 cuts the answer to an excerpt by this view, so it holds only where the view cuts every kind.

One model: one rule of the cut for every kind, at the top as beneath it, under a tag as under none. Four exceptions leave the rule.

For a fresh reader there is one form of a cut: the first parts and a marker of the rest, read by `drop`. Every part a view shows stands at the path that reads it in the data, so a path read off a view is a path of the data. A tail beside the head would need two things this form avoids:
- a marker before the parts kept, which moves their places off those of the data;
- two keys for a map's rest.

The key `:"…"` has one exact sense and wakes the habit of an ellipsis, and no source of the language writes it.
- A key of the data standing for the rest would give that key a second sense.
- It would also wake the habit today's view teaches, where a marker at a key means the value of that key gave way.

A value under a tag cut as its payload shows the first parts of a tagged answer. A marker in its place would tell its size alone.


**Rationale.** Two deciders take the view that cuts every kind, and the third keeps the view as it stands. The 2/3 road also carries the maintainer's own «элайд надо сделать таким что б тот резать мог все», and part 5's refusal needs it: its head is a view that cuts every kind.

**Decision.** The alert keeps its form.
