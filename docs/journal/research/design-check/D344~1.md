## 2. Where a failure broke is spelled three ways

Agreement 3/3, size L.

**Road.** One spelling, the frame's place (the record's first road), extended to a step that refuses a part of its subject. The facts `:index` and `:key` leave every refusal at a part of the subject. `:index` stays for an element of a value no frame holds, and a read of a path keeps its own facts.


**Decision.** Where a step failed at a part of its subject is spelled once, by the place of its frame. The place is the path of that part, read from the subject the frame holds. It covers three cases:
- The code the step ran on the part failed: `map`, `filter`, `every`, `any` and `reduce`, and the keys of `sort`, `min`, `max`, `groupBy` and `indexBy`.
- The step refused what its code answered for the part: the verdict of `filter`, `every` and `any` that is no boolean, and the key of `groupBy` and `indexBy` that files under no keyword or under one another value took.
- The step refused the part itself:
  - an element of `sum` or `avg` that is no number;
  - an element of `zip` that is no vector;
  - an element of `join` that is no text;
  - an element of `toPath` that is no key;
  - an element of a fold of the algebra that is no set or map;
  - an element of `toMap` that is no pair (at `/i`), and the key of a pair (at `/i/0`);
  - a key that a fold of `minus` or `inter` reads, at its path within the element;
  - a row of the command line's `table` that is no map.

`update` names the path it wrote.

A refusal at a part holds no `:index` and no `:key`. Its `:actualValue` holds what it refused: the part itself, or the answer for it.

The facts that stay name what no frame holds:
- `:index` names an element of a value no frame holds as its subject: an input's value, a payload, the args, or the conditions of `allOf`, `anyOf` and `cond`.
- `:key`, beside `:taken`, names the keyword `KeyTakenError` files under.
- A read of a path keeps `:path`, the path it followed, and `:key` or `:index`, the segment it missed.

Each level names the part of its own subject, so `trail | map ~place` reads the breadcrumbs:

#### 7. The step of a frame is a quote

**Road.** The word is renamed for the quote it answers (the record's second road): `step` of `::frame` becomes `failed`, beside `skipped`.

#### 2. Where a failure broke is spelled three ways

**Road.** The record's first road, one spelling: the frame's place, extended to a step that refuses a part of its subject. The facts `:index` and `:key` leave every refusal at an element of the subject. `:index` stays for an element of a value an input, a payload or the args hold, and `:key` and `:path` stay for what a read of a path missed.


**Decision.** A step that breaks at a part of its subject names the part in its frame by its path, `place`. The part is one of three things:
- the element or the entry where the code of `map`, `filter`, `every`, `any` or `reduce`, or the key of `sort`, `min`, `max`, `groupBy` or `indexBy`, broke;
- the path `update` wrote;
- the element the step refuses itself, or whose code's answer it refuses: `sum`'s and `avg`'s element that is no number, the verdict of `filter`, `every` and `any` that is no boolean, `zip`'s element that is no vector, `toMap`'s pair and its key, an element a fold of the algebra reads that is no set or map, `join`'s element that is no text, `toPath`'s element that is no key, and the value whose key `groupBy` or `indexBy` cannot file or files where another value took its keyword. `KeyTakenError` holds that keyword as `:key` and the value that took it as `:taken`.
So `trail | last | place` names the part at the level that reads the failure, whichever broke it, and the frame's subject reads the part, `get (f | place)`. A refusal at a part of its subject carries no `:index` and no `:key`. `:index` is the index of an element of a value an input, a payload or the args hold, where no frame reaches. `:key` and `:path` are what a read of a path missed.


**Why.** One fact, one spelling (AIM, principles and state 3). One element's place is spelled two ways today, by one word: `verdictOf` in core/src/runtime/vec.mjs hands `{ index }` to the fact where the verdict is no boolean, and `placeOf(place)` to the frame where the predicate failed. #### 7. The step of a frame is a quote

**Road.** As it stands: `step` answers the quote of the step that broke, the step as code. The road where it answers the record and the road where it is renamed are both set aside.


**Decision.** `step` answers the step that broke as the code that ran there, the quote of its one step: `"x" | add 1 | mul 2 !| trail | first | step` answers `~(add 1)`. In the same way `skipped` answers the steps left unrun as a quote. So the words of a frame answer its code, which prints as it was written and runs by `apply`; `step | first` reads the record of that step, a `::call` [QSQUO#step]. The word names the step of the pipeline, the one concept a reader of a trail asks for (`!| trail | map ~step`), and its address `::frame/step` is the handle a search finds.


**Why.** Words carry paradigms (aim, principles): a word stays where the habit it wakes is the right one. A reader of a failure asks for the step that failed and reads it as code, like the line of a traceback. The record of a step prints as a record, `::call{:name :add :modifiers [1]}`, while the quote prints as the code written, the signal per character the aim asks of a default answer. The maintainer's principle prefers the distinctive word when other things are equal; here they are not equal. The distinctive candidates weighed were `broken` (7 matches across core/src, core/lib, cli/src, lsp/src, docs/spec and core/test, `git grep -w -i -o broken … | wc -l`, and the chapter's own “the first broken”) and `culprit` (1). `broken` would read as a boolean beside `empty` and `ok`, and `culprit` names a blame the frame of `map` does not carry. A fresh session guesses `step` on its first attempt. The two forms of one step, its code and its record, are the language's own. The maintainer's concern was `stop` against `step`, and the frame resolved it.


**Cost.** None in the tree: the page of `::frame/step` already says “the quote of its one step”. The record moves the alternatives into what it set aside.


**Removes.** None.
