qlang is a pipeline language over values. A query starts from a subject, the input it is given, and passes it through steps: each step takes the value the step before answered and answers a new one, and the last answer is the answer of the query. Nothing changes in place. Everything the language handles is a value with a literal that prints and reads back as itself, data, code, prose and failures alike, so the language describes itself in its own values: every name has a page, the pages grow from ::qlang, and a query reads them as it reads any data.

      1. The pipe. `a | b` runs b on the value a answered, its subject: `[3 1 2] | sort | first` answers `1`.

      2. Values. Every value has a kind, a tag that `type` answers: `[1 2] | type` answers `::vec`. Numbers, ::number, are finite doubles: `42`, `-1.5`. Strings, ::string: `"text"`. Keywords, ::keyword, are names that stand for themselves: `:name`. Booleans and null: `true`, `false`, `null`. Vectors, ::vec: `[1 2 3]`. Maps, ::map, have keyword keys: `{:a 1 :b 2}`. Sets, ::set, hold no duplicates and keep one order: `#[3 1 3]` answers `#[1 3]`. JSON is a literal too and pastes into a query as it is, its keys read as keywords: `{"a": [1, 2]}` answers `{:a [1 2]}`. A keyword is not its string: `:a | eq "a"` answers `false`, and `keyword` turns either into the other, `:a | keyword` answering `"a"`.

      3. Reading parts. `/k` reads a key, `/0` an element, `/-1` the last, `/a/b/0` a path, `/` the whole value: `{:a {:b [10 20]}} | /a/b/-1` answers `20`. A part that is not there is a failure. `at` reads softly and answers null where the part is missing, and `coalesce` puts a fallback in its place: `{:a 1} | at :b | coalesce 0` answers `0`.

      4. Commands. A command is a word and its modifiers, separated by spaces: `add 1`, `split ","`, `take 2`. The word is found through the kind of the subject: `count` counts the elements of a vector and the characters of a string. Every modifier is computed once, at the call, against the subject of the step, and the word receives the values: `10 | add 3` answers `13`, and `{:price 4 :qty 3} | mulOf /price /qty` answers `12`, both modifiers read from the record in the pipe. The subject of a step is what the step before it answered, so in `{:price 4 :qty 3} | /price | mul /qty` the `/qty` is read from the price, a number with no parts, and the call is refused, `::proj/SubjectNotProjectableError`: two values of one record meet in one step, `mulOf /price /qty`. A modifier of more than one word stands in parentheses: `[1 2 3] | take (count | sub 1)` answers `[1 2]`.

      5. Code. A quote, `~(…)`, is code, and a quote computes to itself, so the word receives it unrun and runs it where it needs. `map` runs its code on each element, that element its subject: `[1 2 3] | map ~(mul 2)` answers `[2 4 6]`. `filter` keeps the elements its code holds of, `[1 2 3] | filter ~(gt 1)` answering `[2 3]`; `sort` ranks by what its code answers, `[{:n 2} {:n 1}] | sort ~(/n)` answering `[{:n 1} {:n 2}]`; `groupBy` files the elements under what its code answers, `["b" "a" "b"] | groupBy ~(/) | map ~(count)` answering `{:b 2 :a 1}`; `reduce` folds the elements into a seed, calling its word with the value so far as the subject and the element as the modifier, `[1 2 3] | reduce 0 ~(add)` answering `6`. Several steps stand in one quote, `map ~(/price | mul 2)`. Without the quote the same text is a modifier computed at the call: in `[{:n 1}] | map /n` the `/n` is read from the vector itself, not from each element, and `map` refuses what it is handed, `::vec/map/code/NotQuoteError`.

      6. Building answers. A literal is a step too: it replaces the value in the pipe, and each part inside it is computed against that value, so an answer is reshaped by writing the shape it takes: `{:first "Ann" :age 30} | {:who /first :next (/age | add 1)}` answers `{:who "Ann" :next 31}`, and `{:a 1 :b 2} | [/b /a]` answers `[2 1]`.

      7. Names. A step sees only the value handed to it: after `/qty |` the subject is a number, and the record it came from is gone unless a name keeps it. `:r /` names the value in the pipe `r`, and `:n (…)` names what its group computes; the value in the pipe goes on unchanged, and the bare word reads the name back, `:r` staying a keyword: `{:qty 2 :price 3} | :r / | /qty | mul (r | /price)` answers `6`. A name is declared once in a scope, and the names a group or a quote declares stay in it.

      8. Elements and order. `count`, `sum`, `avg`, `min`, `max`, `first` and `last` read the elements of a vector, a set or a map. A map's elements are its values, the keys travelling with them: `{:a 1 :b 5} | filter ~(gt 2)` answers `{:b 5}`, and `{:a 1 :b 5} | map ~(add 1)` answers `{:a 2 :b 6}`. `keys` answers its keys, and `entries` the pairs `[key value]`, which `tag ::map` builds back into a map. One order ranks every value, so anything sorts: null, then booleans, numbers, strings, keywords, vectors, sets, maps. `desc` turns a key to descend: `sort ~([(/n | desc) /name])` ranks by n descending, then by name.

      9. Conditions. A condition is a boolean, and nothing else is truthy: `filter` and `if` refuse any other value. `if` takes the condition as a modifier and its two branches as code: `5 | if (gt 0) ~("pos") ~("neg")` answers `"pos"`. Conditions of one value hold together by `allOf` and `anyOf`, whose modifiers are computed at the call, every one of them, so a condition that may fail is guarded by a filter before it: `filter ~(/email | ne null) | filter ~(/email | endsWith ".org")`.

      10. Text. A string splits into a vector and joins back, `"a,b,c" | split "," | join "-"` answering `"a-b-c"`; `lines` breaks text into its lines, and `trim`, `lower`, `upper`, `contains`, `startsWith` and `endsWith` read it. A string is never read as a number behind the reader's back: `parseJson` reads one, `"42" | parseJson | add 1` answering `43`.

      11. Failures. A step that fails answers a failure, a value under the tag of what refused: `"x" | add 1 !| type` answers `::number/add/SubjectNotNumberError`. `|` passes a failure by, and `!|` runs its step only on a failure: `"x" | add 1 !| 0` answers `0`. A failure carries its facts and its trail, the steps it passed with their subjects, and prints as an alert: the subject where it arose cut to an excerpt, and each subject it passed an ::elision, whose `:read` continues the query to it, `!| /trail/1/subject`. `!| explain` answers the page of what refused above the failure, the page saying how to repair the call.

      12. Asking. `::vec | manifest` answers the words of a kind, and `map` asks several kinds at once, each beside its words: ::snippet~([::vec ::map ::string ::number ::any] | map ~([/ manifest])) lists the words most queries use. `::vec/sort | doc` answers the page of a word, and `::vec/sort | laws` the laws of a word by name, each a page and a query that answers true; `x | type | doc` answers the page of the kind of any value. An answer prints whole; `x | elide 4000` bounds it to four thousand characters of its print, each part left out an ::elision whose `:read` continues the query to it.

      13. A task. Over `{"users": [{"name": "Ann", "age": 30, "tags": ["a", "b"]}, {"name": "Bo", "age": 15, "tags": []}]}`, `/users | filter ~(/age | gte 18) | map ~({:name /name :n (/tags | count)})` answers `[{:name "Ann" :n 2}]`. Look at the input before shaping it, `keys`, `/0`, `type`, `count`, and build the query a step at a time, running each.
